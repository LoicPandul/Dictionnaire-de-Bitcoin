Model for understanding how information (transactions and blocks) spreads through the network of Bitcoin nodes. Percolation theory is originally a mathematical and physical model that studies the movement and filtration of fluids through porous materials. It analyzes how, beyond a certain threshold, a connected network lets the fluid flow continuously through the material. The theory can be applied to computer networks to see how information spreads, by treating nodes as sites that are either active or inactive. In Bitcoin, nodes thus play the role of the pores in percolation theory. Each active node receives information and passes it on to other nodes, which either continue the transmission or block it. The propagation of certain types of transactions can be analyzed in terms of percolation thresholds, where a certain percentage of active nodes is needed to reach a miner who will include the transaction in a block. This theory provides a framework for evaluating how changes in the network, such as some nodes modifying their standardness rules, affect the cascading propagation by which transactions reach a miner.
