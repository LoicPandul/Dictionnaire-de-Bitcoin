Principle by which a user can credibly deny owning or controlling certain bitcoin, because several plausible interpretations of a given situation exist. In the context of Bitcoin, plausible deniability is a goal pursued by many privacy techniques.

For example, a coinjoin transaction offers plausible deniability because an observer cannot prove with certainty which output belongs to which participant. If a user is asked whether they own a particular UTXO from a coinjoin, they can credibly deny it, because several participants could be its owner.

Likewise, a hidden wallet protected by a BIP-0039 passphrase offers plausible deniability. A user under duress can reveal their mnemonic phrase without the passphrase, giving access to a decoy wallet, while denying the existence of the hidden wallet associated with the secret passphrase.

Plausible deniability is an important concept for protecting the privacy and physical security of bitcoin holders, particularly against state or criminal adversaries.
