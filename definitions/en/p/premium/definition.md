Additional amount paid above the standard or nominal price of an asset. In the context of Bitcoin, a premium can be observed in purchases, notably on exchanges, which can sometimes use this technique to make their margin on the brokerage service.

The notion of premium also appears in peer-to-peer purchases of BTC. Indeed, buying non-KYC bitcoin often comes with a premium over the standard market price, which can range from 1% to sometimes more than 10%. Several reasons explain this price difference. First, it is a common practice among P2P sellers that has settled in over time. Next, sellers have costs associated with the transaction of sending the funds to the buyer. There is also a higher risk of theft in P2P sales compared with transactions on regulated platforms, which justifies compensation for the risk taken. Finally, the extra cost can be tied to demand and to the quality of the trade in terms of privacy. For a buyer, the privacy gained has a price that is reflected in the markup applied by the seller. Some bitcoiners also believe that the marked-up price of BTC bought P2P reflects its true price, and argue that the lower prices on regulated platforms result from a compromise on the privacy of users' personal data.
