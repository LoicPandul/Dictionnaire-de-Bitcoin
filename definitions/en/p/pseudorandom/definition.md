Adjective describing a sequence of numbers that, although the result of a deterministic process, displays characteristics close to those of an ideal, truly random sequence. The notion of ideal randomness implies a total absence of predictability and of correlation between successive elements. A pseudorandom number is generated by a deterministic algorithm and is therefore, in theory, entirely predictable if the initial state of the generator is known.

A pseudorandom number generator (PRNG) is an algorithm used to produce such numbers. It generally starts from an initial value, or “seed,” and then applies a series of mathematical transformations to produce the sequence of numbers. Because of this determinism, it is important for cryptographic security that the initial seed remain secret. Pseudorandom sequences are widely used in various fields, notably cryptography, because they exhibit a seemingly random behavior that is sufficient for many applications. The quality of a PRNG is evaluated by how closely its output approximates true randomness in terms of distribution, correlations, and other statistical properties. In Bitcoin, pseudorandom numbers are used to produce private keys, or to produce a seed for hierarchical deterministic wallets.
