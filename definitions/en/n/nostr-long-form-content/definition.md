Long text published on Nostr, an article or blog post, in the form of an addressable event of kind `30023` defined by NIP-23, whose content is written in Markdown.

An ordinary note event is poorly suited to an article: kind `1` is designed for short text, and the event cannot be corrected. NIP-23 therefore uses an addressable kind, from the range `30000` to `39999`. For the same author, the same kind, and the same value of the `d` tag, an identifier chosen by the author, relays must keep the latest version and may discard the previous ones, which makes the article editable. A client reading the article should hide older versions received from a relay that does not handle this mechanism.

The `content` field contains the text in Markdown. NIP-23 imposes two rules on clients that create an article: do not break paragraphs with arbitrary line breaks, for example every 80 characters, and do not accept HTML in the Markdown, to stay compatible and readable from one client and device to another. Metadata goes through tags. Four metadata tags are standardized and optional: `title` for the title, `image` for an illustration, `summary` for the summary, and `published_at` for the date of first publication, in Unix seconds. The `created_at` field of the event keeps the date of the last update, and topics are `t` tags.

Take an example. Alice publishes an article about multisig: an event of kind `30023` with a `d` tag equal to `guide-multisig`, a `title` tag equal to “Multisig guide”, and a `content` that is her text in Markdown. A week later, she fixes an error: her client publishes a new event with the same `d` tag, which replaces the previous one on relays. To share the article, she circulates an NADDR, which designates the article by its coordinate, without citing the ID of a specific version, and therefore leads to the latest version. In another event, an `a` tag can also reference it by its coordinate. References to other notes or profiles inside the text use the `nostr:` links of NIP-21, as described in NIP-27.

Replies to an article should not be kind `1` notes but NIP-22 comments, of kind `1111`. NIP-23 also notes that social clients centered on kind `1` cannot be expected to handle these articles. Kind `30024`, which was used for drafts, is obsolete: NIP-37 defines the recommended way to write them.

NIP-23 was added to the NIP repository on February 3, 2023, by fiatjaf, the day NIP-19 received the identifier that would become the NADDR, then named `nref`. The mention of NIP-22 comments was added on March 6, 2025.
