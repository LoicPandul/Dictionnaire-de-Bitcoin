Remote signer defined by NIP-46: program that keeps a Nostr user's private key on a machine or device separate from the client, and signs the user's events by answering requests carried by relays. NIP-46 names it remote signer and specifies that it is also called bunker. It can run on a phone, on a server, or in a dedicated hardware device, and it suits a client that can use neither a browser extension nor a local application (see NOSTR SIGNER).

The protocol distinguishes 3 key pairs. The client generates its own: a throwaway key used to encrypt its exchanges with the bunker, which it deletes on disconnection. The bunker has its own for the same exchanges, which may be identical to the user's key without having to be. The user's key is the one that actually signs events, and that the bunker controls. The client discovers it by calling `get_public_key` after connecting.

The exchanges go through events of kind `24133`. For a request, the client signs with its throwaway key an event that carries a `p` tag pointing to the bunker's key and, in `content`, a JSON message encrypted with NIP-44 in the form `{id, method, params}`. The bunker answers with an event of the same kind, signed with its key, with a `p` tag pointing to the client and an encrypted message `{id, result, error}`. These kinds are in the ephemeral range (`20000` to `29999`): NIP-01 does not expect relays to store them. The main methods are `connect`, `get_public_key`, `sign_event`, `ping`, the four encryption and decryption methods (`nip04_encrypt`, `nip04_decrypt`, `nip44_encrypt`, `nip44_decrypt`), `switch_relays`, and `logout`.

To establish the connection, NIP-46 provides for 2 directions. In the first, the bunker provides a URL of the form `bunker://<bunker-key>?relay=wss://…&secret=…`, where the `secret` is optional. The user pastes it into the client, which sends `connect` through the indicated relays. A secret is good for only one established connection, and the bunker should ignore later attempts with the same secret. In the second, the client displays a `nostrconnect://<client-key>` URL with at least one `relay` and one `secret`, and optionally the requested permissions (`perms`), its name, its address, and an image. The user gives it to the bunker, which answers on the client's relays; the client must check that the returned `secret` matches its own, otherwise someone could impersonate the bunker.

Let's take an example. Alice uses Amber on her phone. She opens a web client, chooses the bunker connection, and pastes the `bunker://` URL that Amber gave her. The client generates a throwaway key pair, sends `connect` and then `get_public_key` through the relays in the URL, and learns Alice's key. When she publishes a note, it sends `sign_event` with an event of kind `1`. Amber checks Alice's permissions, signs, returns the event, and the client publishes it itself. Alice's key never left her phone.

The bunker keeps control of the relays: with `switch_relays`, it sends back its updated list or `null`, and the client switches to it, which makes it possible to replace a relay that has disappeared. When it needs outside authentication, it answers `auth_url` with an address that the client opens in a window. The content of the exchanges is encrypted, but relays see the keys of both parties and the dates of the events. A bunker can also make itself known through the `nip46` field of its `nostr.json` file (NIP-05) or through a NIP-89 event (kind `31990`); see NIP-05 identifier.

Several signers offer it. Amber has offered bunker mode since its version 1.0.0 of May 10, 2024 (the beta of April 17, 2024 already announced it), and nsec.app (see nsec.app) runs its bunker in a browser service worker, which a server wakes up by notification when a request arrives.

NIP-46 was added to the repository on February 20, 2023 under the title “Nostr Connect” (PR by Marco Argentieri). The `bunker://` URL replaced the old notation on February 6, 2024, the distinction between the bunker's key and the user's key was introduced on October 24, 2024, and the encryption of the exchanges moved from NIP-04 to NIP-44 on December 5, 2024 (the `nip04_encrypt` and `nip04_decrypt` methods are still offered to the client). The `logout` method was added on June 14, 2026.

*Not to be confused with a browser extension or a local Android application, which are also signers but do not use relays.*
