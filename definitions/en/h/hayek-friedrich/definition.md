Economist and philosopher born in Vienna, a leading figure of the Austrian school. He joined the London School of Economics in the early 1930s at the invitation of Lionel Robbins, and stayed there for eighteen years. He published *The Road to Serfdom* in 1944. In 1974, he shared the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel with Gunnar Myrdal, for their pioneering work on the theory of money and economic fluctuations.

In 1976, the Institute of Economic Affairs in London published his *Denationalisation of Money*. Hayek argues there that the state monopoly on money should be abolished: private institutions would each issue their own, and stability of value would decide which ones the public would choose. He published a revised edition in 1978. The legal tender and Gresham's law entries touch on his subject.

He died in Freiburg im Breisgau in 1992 and did not live to see Bitcoin. Bitcoin advocates invoke his proposal of competing currencies, but a 2024 historical study concludes that no direct line can yet be traced between the book and Bitcoin, only an influence on the libertarian current from which the cryptocurrency emerged. His name appears in the Austrian school entry.
