Historic event of the Blocksize War, held on February 20, 2016, in Hong Kong. It was an important meeting between Bitcoin Core developers and miners to discuss Bitcoin's scalability and the strategy to scale it. Tensions were high before the meeting, notably because of the rise of Bitcoin Classic, a hard fork proposal backed by Gavin Andresen that aimed to raise the block size to 2 MB. Influential figures of the ecosystem took part in the discussions, including Jihan Wu and Micree Zhan, the co-founders of Bitmain, Adam Back, the president of Blockstream, as well as Luke Dashjr, Matt Corallo, and Peter Todd.

The miners, frustrated by the lack of progress, threatened to support Bitcoin Classic if a 2 MB hard fork was not implemented. The developers defended SegWit as a viable solution. After hours of tense negotiations, an agreement was reached, stipulating that Bitcoin Core developers would work on a hard fork after the implementation of SegWit. The agreement was meant to ease tensions, but it ended up creating more distrust and confusion. Each side interpreted it differently, which deepened the divisions within the community. Although the agreement temporarily slowed the momentum of Bitcoin Classic, many saw it as an unsatisfactory and clumsy solution.
