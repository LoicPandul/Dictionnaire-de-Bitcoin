Lightning channel that is not anchored in an on-chain transaction, unlike regular channels. A hosted channel, also called a "custodial channel" or "virtual channel", does not rely on any funding transaction on the Bitcoin blockchain: it exists only at the Lightning protocol level, with no guarantee of on-chain settlement.

It involves two asymmetric roles: a host, which provides the channel with liquidity on its side, and a client, which uses the channel to access the Lightning Network. Funds remain in the host's custody, which makes the model custodial. Compared with traditional custodial wallets, however, hosted channels offer additional guarantees: the client generates its own invoices and controls the payment preimages, which makes fraud detectable. The host and the client exchange mutually signed state data, which provides cryptographic proof of the balance owed. Opening and closing these channels costs nothing in mining fees, which makes it cheap to onboard new users onto the Lightning Network.
