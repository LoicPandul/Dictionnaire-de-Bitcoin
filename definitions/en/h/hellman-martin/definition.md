American cryptographer and professor, coauthor with Whitfield Diffie of "New Directions in Cryptography" (November 1976), the paper that founded public-key cryptography and described the key exchange that bears both their names. Born in October 1945 in New York, he holds a doctorate in electrical engineering from Stanford University (1969), worked at IBM in 1968-1969, taught at MIT from 1969 to 1971, then at Stanford from 1971 to 1996, where he became professor emeritus.

In 1974, Diffie came to see him at Stanford, and the two researchers worked together on a problem that nobody had publicly posed: how two correspondents could agree on a secret key without a courier or a key distribution center. The paper, received by *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* on June 3, 1976, proposes a key exchange based on modular exponentiation, whose security rests on the difficulty of the discrete logarithm problem, and describes the idea of a digital signature produced with a private key and verified with a public key. Hellman also supervised the thesis of Ralph Merkle, who had independently conceived a public-key distribution scheme, and maintains that the key exchange should be called Diffie-Hellman-Merkle. A patent on the key exchange was issued to Diffie, Hellman, and Merkle on April 29, 1980.

In the late 1970s, he defended the right of researchers to publish their cryptography work and criticized DES, a standard adopted on November 23, 1976, with a 56-bit key that he considered too short. He received the 2015 Turing Award with Diffie, announced in March 2016, "for inventing and promulgating both asymmetric public-key cryptography, including its application to digital signatures, and a practical cryptographic key-exchange method."

Bitcoin uses the elliptic-curve variant of this exchange, ECDH. In the v2 P2P transport of BIP-0324, two nodes derive from it the keys that encrypt their connection. His digital signature, which remained an idea in the 1976 paper, is the principle that lets a bitcoin owner authorize a spend with a private key.
