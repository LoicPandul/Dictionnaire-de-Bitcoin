Programmer, cofounder of the cypherpunks and author of their manifesto.

In September 1992, he organized with Timothy May the first cypherpunks meeting in the San Francisco area; John Gilmore then lent them his offices for the monthly meetings. The mailing list was born right away, set up with the help of Hugh Daniel on Gilmore's machine toad.com; Hughes was its administrator from the start. The same year, he wrote the first cypherpunk remailer, a relay that strips the sender's address before forwarding the mail: a rudimentary version, without encryption or shuffling, of David Chaum's “mixes,” according to Timothy May.

On March 9, 1993, he signed “A Cypherpunk's Manifesto.” The text distinguishes privacy from secrecy: privacy is the power to selectively reveal oneself to the world. He concludes that an open society requires anonymous transaction systems, of which cash has so far been the main example, and cryptography, digital signatures included. Since governments and large organizations cannot be expected to grant it, everyone must defend their own privacy, and all together must create these systems. Hence the motto “Cypherpunks write code”: cypherpunks write the software themselves and publish it for everyone to use.

This is the program that Bitcoin later took up: an electronic money that does without intermediaries.
