Economic phenomenon characterized by an extremely rapid and uncontrollable rise in prices. Hyperinflation occurs when a government or central bank issues money in excessive quantities, often to finance budget deficits, leading to a total loss of confidence in the national currency.

The most striking historical examples are the Weimar Republic in Germany (1923), Zimbabwe in the 2000s, and Venezuela from 2016. In these cases, the currency lost its function as a store of value and a medium of exchange, pushing populations toward alternative currencies or barter.

The risk of hyperinflation is a central argument for bitcoiners, who point to the supply of BTC, capped at 21 million units, as protection against this kind of monetary excess. Unlike fiat currencies, no authority can arbitrarily increase the amount of bitcoin in circulation.
