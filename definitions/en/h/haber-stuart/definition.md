Cryptographer and computer scientist, coinventor with W. Scott Stornetta of digital timestamping by hash chaining, three of whose papers are among the references of the Bitcoin white paper ([3], [4], and [5]). He carried out this work at the Bellcore laboratory in Morristown, New Jersey.

In 1991, Haber and Stornetta published "How To Time-Stamp a Digital Document" in the *Journal of Cryptology*, received by the journal on August 19, 1990. The problem: proving the date on which a digital document was created or modified, without trusting the service that timestamps it. Alice sends the service the hash of her document, not the document itself, which stays private. The service returns a signed certificate containing that hash, the time, and linking information drawn from the previous certificate, including the hash of its own linking information. Each certificate thus depends on those that precede it: to backdate or postdate a document, the service would have to find a collision in the hash function or fabricate a fake chain long enough to withstand any check. U.S. Patent 5,136,646, covering the method, was issued to them on August 4, 1992.

In 1993, with Dave Bayer, they published a method that groups timestamps into Merkle trees to reduce the storage and computation needed for verification (the paper is dated March 1992). In April 1997, they published "Secure names for bit-strings" at the ACM CCS conference.

The white paper builds on this chain in its section 3 ("Timestamp Server"): a timestamp server publishes the hash of a block of data, and each timestamp includes the previous one in its hash, forming a chain. Bitcoin replaces the single server with a peer-to-peer network and proof of work, but each block remains linked to the previous one by its hash.
