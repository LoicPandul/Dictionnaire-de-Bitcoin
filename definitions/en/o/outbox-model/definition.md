Method for finding events on Nostr in which each user declares the relays where they publish (their write relays, or outbox) and those where they receive mentions (their read relays, or inbox), and in which clients fetch the user's notes from those relays rather than from the ones they are already connected to. Mike Dilger, the author of the Gossip client, described this method and wrote the first version of NIP-65, which other contributors later reworked and which provides the relay list the method needs.

It addresses a difficulty in Nostr's architecture: an event is found only on the relays where its author sent it (see NOSTR CLIENT). If Alice's client connects to only a few relays chosen once and for all, it does not see the notes Bob publishes elsewhere. With the outbox model, each author states where to read them, and Alice's client connects to the relays of the people she follows.

The indication takes the form of an event of kind `10002` (see REPLACEABLE EVENT, so only one per author, the most recent). It contains `r` tags (see NOSTR TAG), one per relay, with an optional `read` or `write` marker; without a marker, the relay is used for both purposes. Bob can, for example, list `wss://notes.bob.example` as `write` and `wss://inbox.bob.example` as `read`. To read Bob, Alice's client starts from his public key, taken from her follow list, finds his kind `10002` on relays that index it, then downloads his notes from `wss://notes.bob.example`. When Alice replies to Bob, her client sends the reply to her own write relays and to Bob's read relays, so that Bob sees the mention, and attaches her kind `10002` to all the relays used. NIP-65 phrases these rules with “SHOULD”: they guide clients without binding them.

The model does not depend on any particular relay, and anyone can switch relays by republishing their list. NIP-65 recommends keeping it short, with 2 to 4 relays per category, and spreading it across as many relays as is practical, in particular public indexers, where most clients publish and look up these lists. Other hints complete the list: the author's key placed in an `e` tag (NIP-10) is used to look for the event on the author's write relays when the listed relays returned nothing, and an NEVENT or an NPROFILE can carry relays. Private messages follow a separate list, the kind `10050` of NIP-17 (see NOSTR PRIVATE DIRECT MESSAGE).

The model assumes that authors publish an up-to-date kind `10002`, and that clients open connections to many relays instead of a few. NIP-65 also has the status `draft` and `optional` in the NIP repository.

Mike Dilger proposed NIP-65 on February 3, 2023 under the title “Feed Advertisements”: the event then announced the relays where the author publishes their feed. On February 5, he renamed it “Relay List Metadata” and added read relays to it; the NIP was merged into the NIP repository on February 7. Before NIP-65, Gossip determined where people published from five sources: manual entry, `nprofile`, the relay field of NIP-05, relays written in contact lists, and relays recommended in tags; the kind `10002` was added to these afterward. Dilger presents the “gossip model” as an older term for a more general form of the outbox model. Other software has adopted it: the README of NDK (see NDK - NOSTR DEVELOPMENT KIT) lists the outbox model among its relay discovery features, and NIP-10 refers to it.

*The older name, “gossip model”, should not be confused with Lightning's gossip protocol.*
