Cryptographer and cypherpunk computer scientist, author of b-money, which the Bitcoin white paper cites as reference [1] in support of the public announcement of transactions.

In November 1998, he announced on the cypherpunks mailing list a text titled “b-money,” posted on his personal page. Each participant, identified by a pseudonymous public key, keeps their own ledger of the balances of all pseudonyms. Anyone can create money by broadcasting the solution to a hard computational problem, and payments are signed messages. Each party to a contract sets a maximum reparation, placed in escrow, and the contract provides for an arbitrator. A second protocol entrusts the ledgers to servers that put up a deposit, forfeited in case of misconduct. The text remained a proposal: b-money was never put into service.

In 2008, Satoshi Nakamoto wrote to him to find out the year b-money was published, which he wanted to cite in his paper. This kinship explains Wei Dai's place alongside Adam Back's Hashcash, Nick Szabo's bit gold, and Hal Finney's RPOW, other milestones of proof of work and digital money before Bitcoin.

He is also the author of Crypto++, a free cryptography library in C++.
