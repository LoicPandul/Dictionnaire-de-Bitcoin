Company founded in 1989 by cryptographer David Chaum, a pioneer in anonymous electronic payments. DigiCash developed the eCash system, a digital money protocol based on the blind signatures invented by Chaum. This mechanism allowed a bank to issue digital tokens without being able to trace the transactions made by users, guaranteeing anonymity comparable to that of cash. Despite trials with several banks, notably Mark Twain Bank in the United States and Deutsche Bank, the system failed to reach sufficient adoption. DigiCash went bankrupt in 1998. The eCash concept is considered an important precursor of Bitcoin, notably for its approach to privacy in digital payments.
