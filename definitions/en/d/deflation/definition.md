Economic phenomenon characterized by a general and lasting fall in the price level of goods and services. Unlike inflation, deflation means that the purchasing power of money increases over time: with the same amount of money, more goods can be bought.

Deflation can result from a contraction of the money supply, a rise in productivity, or a fall in demand. Keynesian economists generally regard it as harmful, because it would encourage agents to postpone their purchases, thus slowing economic activity. Economists of the Austrian school, however, distinguish monetary deflation (an artificial contraction of credit) from the fall in prices linked to productivity gains, the latter being seen as beneficial.

Bitcoin is often described as "deflationary" because of its supply capped at 21 million units and the gradual reduction of its issuance through halvings. More precisely, it is disinflationary: its issuance rate decreases over time and will converge to zero.
