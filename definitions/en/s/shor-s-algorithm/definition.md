Quantum algorithm invented in 1994 by Peter Shor that factors large integers into products of prime numbers in polynomial time. By reducing the number of operations needed to factor integers, Shor's algorithm could make cryptographic algorithms based on this mathematical problem, such as RSA, impractical. It can be slightly modified to act on almost all algorithms that use a group structure. In particular, an efficient variant already exists for elliptic curve cryptography (ECDSA, Schnorr...). Shor's algorithm and its close variants are therefore effective against asymmetric cryptography algorithms. In 2026, no quantum computer is yet powerful and stable enough to successfully run Shor's algorithm on numbers the size of those used in cryptography.
