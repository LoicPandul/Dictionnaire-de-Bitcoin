Acronym for *Secure Hash Algorithm 256*. It is a cryptographic hash function producing a 256-bit hash digest. Designed by the National Security Agency (NSA) in the early 2000s, it became a federal standard for handling sensitive data. In the Bitcoin protocol, the `SHA256` function is ubiquitous. It is used to hash block headers as part of proof of work. `SHA256` is also used in the process of deriving a receiving address from a public key. It is also used to aggregate transactions and witnesses in the Merkle trees of blocks. `SHA256` also appears in the calculation of key fingerprints, in the calculation of certain checksums, and in many other processes around Bitcoin. When applied twice in a row, the result is called a `HASH256`. This double application is the one used most often on Bitcoin. When `SHA256` is used together with the `RIPEMD160` function, the result is called a `HASH160`. This double hash is used for key fingerprints and for hashing public keys. The `SHA256` function belongs to the SHA-2 family.
