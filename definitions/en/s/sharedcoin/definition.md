Bitcoin coin-mixing service launched in 2013 by Blockchain.info and no longer in operation today. The service let users improve their privacy on Bitcoin by combining their transactions with those of other people, using a mixing technique similar to coinjoins. SharedCoin provided a form of privacy without the coordinator being able to seize the coins, since they stayed under the users' control throughout the process. Unlike the centralized mixing services of the time, the bitcoin could not be stolen by the intermediary. In June 2014, researcher Kristov Atlas showed that the structure of the transactions (the amounts of the inputs and outputs) made it possible to link many inputs to their outputs, which deanonymized some mixes.
