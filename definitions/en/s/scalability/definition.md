Ability of Bitcoin to handle an increase in transaction volume while maintaining acceptable performance. Bitcoin faces inherent technical limitations, such as block size, the time interval between blocks, and the fact that a transaction is considered immutable only after several confirmations. These constraints keep Bitcoin from processing an unlimited number of transactions efficiently. When demand for block space intensifies, users are forced to raise the fees they offer for their transactions to be processed. Scalability, that is, Bitcoin's ability to handle a growing transaction volume, can be improved either by direct changes to the protocol or by external solutions such as the Lightning Network. The question of scalability has always been at the center of heated debates in the community. The first attempt to increase the block size dates to October 2010, with [a patch proposed by Jeff Garzik](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1347.0). This topic of scalability is notably what led to the blocksize war between 2015 and 2017.
