Difficulty target higher than Bitcoin's (and therefore less difficult), set by a mining pool to qualify a hash as a share and evaluate the contribution of each individual miner in the pool. To handle the verification load efficiently and ensure that every miner, whatever their computing power, submits shares regularly, pools adjust the difficulty of shares. The difficulty assigned to each miner determines the number of shares they accumulate: for example, if a miner has an assigned difficulty of 10 and submits 5 valid proofs of work at that difficulty, they get 50 shares. A more powerful miner with a difficulty of 100 who submits 7 valid proofs of work will receive 700 shares. This system precisely quantifies each miner's hashrate and distributes rewards according to the chosen payout method (PPS, PPLNS, TIDES...).
