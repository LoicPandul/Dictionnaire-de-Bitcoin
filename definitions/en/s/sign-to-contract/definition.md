Cryptographic protocol that commits arbitrary data into the nonce of a digital signature, without the commitment being visible from the outside. This technique is the analog of pay-to-contract, but applied to the nonce (the random value $k$) rather than to the public key. The principle relies on tweaking the nonce: from an original nonce $k$ and a commitment $c$, a new nonce $k' = k + H(R \| c)$ is computed, where $R = k \cdot G$ is the public point of the original nonce and $H$ is a hash function. The resulting public nonce $R' = k' \cdot G$ looks like an arbitrary point on the curve, but it encodes a verifiable commitment to $c$. To prove the commitment, it suffices to reveal $R$ and $c$.

This construction has several applications in Bitcoin. In particular, it can timestamp documents: the hash of a file is inserted into the nonce of an ordinary transaction. It is also the underlying mechanism of the anti-exfiltration protocol (anti-exfil), in which a host software injects its own entropy into the nonce of a hardware wallet to prevent the private key from leaking through a covert channel.
