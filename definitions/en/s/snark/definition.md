Cryptographic proof system whose acronym stands for *Succinct Non-interactive ARgument of Knowledge*. A SNARK lets a prover convince a verifier that a statement is true, without interaction and without revealing the underlying data, by producing a very small proof (a few hundred bytes) that can be verified in constant time. This conciseness is the main advantage of SNARKs over STARKs. However, most SNARK constructions, such as Groth16, require a trusted setup ceremony specific to the circuit being proven. If the secret parameters of this ceremony are compromised, it becomes possible to forge false proofs. SNARKs rely on hardness assumptions related to elliptic curves and are therefore not inherently resistant to quantum attacks. In the Bitcoin ecosystem, SNARKs are used in particular to compress larger STARK proofs before their on-chain publication, as in the case of the Citrea rollup.
