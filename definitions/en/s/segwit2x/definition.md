Controversial hard fork attempt aiming to double the block size limit on Bitcoin while also including SegWit. SegWit2x was introduced at the New York Agreement in 2017, a confidential meeting of more than 50 companies from the ecosystem that sought a solution to the system's scaling problem. SegWit2x sought to increase Bitcoin's transaction capacity by raising the maximum block size to 2 MB, up from 1 MB. The hard fork was scheduled for block 494,784, which was due in November 2017.

Despite SegWit's activation in August 2017, the 2X project to double the block size failed to reach consensus, notably because of the “NO2X” opposition campaign, which led to its cancellation in early November. Many users and developers saw the episode as an attack on Bitcoin, in the context of the Blocksize War.

*SegWit2x is sometimes also called “B2X” or “S2X”. Its original name was “SegWit2Mb”.*
