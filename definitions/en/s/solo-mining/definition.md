Mining practice in which a miner uses their own equipment to search for valid proofs of work autonomously, without joining a mining pool. The solo miner runs their own full node to retrieve pending transactions, build candidate blocks, and try to find a hash that meets the network's difficulty target.

In solo mining, the miner keeps the entire block reward when finding a valid block, with no pool fees to pay, and retains full control over the selection of transactions included in the blocks. On the other hand, the probability of finding a block is directly proportional to the miner's hashrate relative to the total hashrate of the network. For an individual miner with a modest hashrate, this probability is extremely low, which makes income very irregular and unpredictable. This high variance is what motivated the emergence of mining pools, where miners pool their computing power to receive more frequent and regular payouts.
