Israeli cryptographer, professor at the Weizmann Institute, Adi Shamir is the inventor of the secret sharing scheme that bears his name and one of the three coinventors of RSA.

With Ron Rivest and Leonard Adleman, he signed the article describing RSA, a public-key encryption and signature system, received by the journal on April 4, 1977 and published in 1978. According to the ACM, he was an instructor and then assistant professor at MIT from 1977 to 1980, before returning to the Weizmann Institute.

In November 1979, *Communications of the ACM* published his article “How to Share a Secret”. It shows how to divide a piece of data into n shares so that any k shares reconstruct it, while k - 1 shares reveal nothing: a threshold scheme. The article proposes using it to manage cryptographic keys, for example so that a company requires at least three signatures from its executives on each check. The method is described in the Shamir Secret Sharing (SSS) entry.

In April 1996, he presented PayWord and MicroMint with Rivest, two micropayment systems that predate Bitcoin. In 2002, he shared the Turing Award with Rivest and Adleman.

In Bitcoin, his name designates the method that splits a seed into several shares: SLIP-39 from SatoshiLabs and BIP-0093 (Codex32) both use it. Losing a few shares then does not cost the seed, and stealing a few does not reveal it, as long as the threshold is not reached.
