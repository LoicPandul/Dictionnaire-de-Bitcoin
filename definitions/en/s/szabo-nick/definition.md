Computer scientist, Nick Szabo is the theorist of the smart contract and the designer of bit gold, one of the digital currency projects that predate Bitcoin.

In 1994, he wrote “Smart Contracts”, a text that defines the smart contract as a computerized transaction protocol that executes the terms of a contract, to reduce exceptions, the need for trusted intermediaries, and the cost of fraud.

He conceived bit gold, by his own account in 1998, and did not describe it in detail publicly until December 29, 2005, on his blog. Each unit of bit gold is a proof of work computed from a challenge string; the proof is timestamped and entered into a distributed registry of property titles, where ownership passes through a chain of digital signatures. The last chain created provides the challenge for the next, which forms a series of linked proofs of work. This design foreshadows the proof of work and the ledger of Bitcoin, which Satoshi Nakamoto's white paper nonetheless does not cite.

On May 28, 2011, in “Bitcoin, what took ye so long?”, he wrote that Nakamoto fixed an important weakness in his design by requiring proof of work to be a node in the peer-to-peer system, to reduce the risk of a dishonest party controlling the majority.
