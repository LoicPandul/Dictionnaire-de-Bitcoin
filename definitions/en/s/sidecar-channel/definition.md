Lightning channel opened by a third party (party A) for the benefit of another user (party B), toward a routing node (party C). This mechanism, introduced in Lightning Pool by Lightning Labs, lets new users join the Lightning Network without needing to hold bitcoin beforehand to fund a channel.

In practice, a node operator with a funded Pool account can buy a channel lease in the name of a third party. The selling routing node then opens a channel directly to the beneficiary, who can immediately receive payments through a peer well connected to the network. This model creates a source of revenue for intermediary operators, while supporting the decentralization of the network, since new users connect through a diversity of peers rather than a single point. Sidecar channels also make it possible to create dual-funded channels and can be deployed as turbo channels, without waiting for on-chain confirmations.
