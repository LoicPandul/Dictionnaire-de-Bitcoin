In a mining pool, a stale share is a share (a unit of work) submitted by a miner after a new block has already been found, which makes the work done on the old block obsolete. This usually happens because of network latency between the miner and the pool server: the miner keeps computing hashes on a block that is no longer current, because it has not yet received the notification of the new block.

Stale shares are rejected by the pool and are not counted in the calculation of the miner's payout. A stale share rate of around 1% is considered normal. A higher rate can point to network connection problems, excessive latency between the miner and the pool server, or a very high hashrate that generates shares faster than the network can transmit them. To reduce the stale share rate, miners can favor an Ethernet connection over Wi-Fi, choose a pool that is geographically close to their equipment, or use a mining proxy.
