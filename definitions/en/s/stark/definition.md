Acronym for *Scalable Transparent ARgument of Knowledge*. Cryptographic proof system that lets a prover demonstrate the validity of a computation to a verifier, without revealing the underlying data and without requiring a trusted setup. This "transparency" property sets STARKs apart from SNARKs, which generally require a prior setup ceremony. STARKs rely on hash functions and on the FRI protocol (Fast Reed-Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity), which makes them resistant to quantum attacks. In return, STARK proofs are significantly larger than SNARK proofs (on the order of several tens of kB), which makes them less suited to direct on-chain verification. To work around this limitation, it is common to wrap a STARK proof in a more compact SNARK proof before publishing it. This is the approach taken by the Citrea rollup on Bitcoin, through the RISC Zero framework.
