Revenue an issuer of money earns from the difference between the face value of the money it produces and its cost of production. Historically, seigniorage referred to the profit made by a lord or sovereign when minting metal coins: the cost of the metal and of minting was lower than the nominal value of the coin put into circulation.

In the modern fiat monetary system, seigniorage takes on considerable scale. Central banks create money at almost zero cost (through a simple accounting entry or printing), while that money circulates at its nominal value. This mechanism amounts to an implicit tax on holders of existing money, whose purchasing power is diluted by each new issuance.

In Bitcoin, there is no seigniorage in the traditional sense. The issuance of new bitcoin through the block subsidy does not benefit a central authority, but pays miners in return for a real cost: the energy and hardware needed for mining. Eventually, the block subsidy will reach zero.
