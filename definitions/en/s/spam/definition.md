Term in common use, absent from the protocol rules, for the transactions or data that a person considers unwanted on Bitcoin. The word has no technical definition: Bitcoin Core's code does not distinguish any “spam” category in its relay rules (`src/policy/` does not contain the word), and only three comments in `src/validation.cpp` use it, about witness data and block headers. What the word covers therefore depends on who uses it. The debates on the subject concern what is valid under consensus and what nodes agree to relay by default, two distinct things (see RELAY POLICY).

Depending on the thread, the word refers to inscriptions, BRC-20 tokens, data placed in `OP_RETURN`, or small outputs that bloat the UTXO set. Inscriptions have a dated starting point: Casey Rodarmor made the first one on mainnet on December 14, 2022, then enabled the `inscribe` command on January 9, 2023. He chose Taproot scripts because they had no size limit. Their content is stored in a script revealed during a script path spend, and the Ordinals handbook specifies that it benefits from the discount granted to witness data (see INSCRIPTIONS and WITNESS DISCOUNT). According to BIP-0141, a witness byte weighs 1 weight unit against 4 for any other byte.

Positions revolve around two questions: can a relay rule discourage these uses, and should action be taken at the consensus level?

On the first, Antoine Poinsot wrote on the bitcoindev mailing list on April 17, 2025 that standardness rules serve to limit denial-of-service risks, to leave room for upgrades, or to discourage certain uses, and that the `OP_RETURN` limit falls into the third category. In his words, “the nudge is ineffective to deter storing data onchain”. Luke Dashjr replied on April 26: “The bugs should be fixed, not the abuse embraced”, and he proposed, if the filters are bypassed, going back to a whitelist. The same day, Pieter Wuille declared himself in favor of loosening the limit after having defended it in the past: blocks are full with or without these transactions, and demand for them will find private channels to miners. On April 28, Jason Hughes wrote that “Bitcoin is not file storage” and feared that lifting the limit would allow arbitrary data to be stored in the clear and in a single piece on every full node. The Bitcoin Core 30.0 release notes, dated October 10, 2025, state that `-datacarriersize` becomes 100,000 by default, which lifts the limit in practice. They also allow several `OP_RETURN` outputs per transaction (see DATACARRIERSIZE). PR #32406, which introduces this change, was merged on June 9, 2025, while Peter Todd's (#32359) was closed.

On the second, Wuille notes that consensus changes are “not optional, apply to everyone equally”. BIP-0110, proposed by Dathon Ohm (number assigned on December 3, 2025), provides for a temporary one-year soft fork that limits the size of certain data fields. Its text asserts that “policy is still the best place to fight spam”, and according to its changelog it was marked *Closed* on August 9, 2026, following a chain split with stalled mining.

The word itself is contested. On Delving Bitcoin, ProofOfKeags wrote on January 8, 2024 that “the notion of 'spam' is incoherent in a decentralized system”, while other participants in the same thread use it, moonsettler for BRC-20 tokens and GregTonoski, who opens the thread, in calling for a change to the witness discount. The user sipa describes the discount there as an attempt to discourage the bytes that cost validating nodes more. On May 20, 2025, RobinLinus uses the word for outputs of less than 1,000 sats and estimates that half of the UTXO set is probably tied to them, which leads him to propose expiring old small outputs (see DUST).

*The word is also used for repeated sends of unwanted messages, such as the low-work block headers that a Bitcoin Core comment mentions, a sense distinct from that of the debates over arbitrary data.*
