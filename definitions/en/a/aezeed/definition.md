Mnemonic phrase format specific to LND, used to generate and back up the private keys of the on-chain wallet associated with a Lightning node. Like BIP-0039, aezeed produces a 24-word mnemonic phrase, but it fixes two major limitations of that standard: the lack of versioning and the lack of a wallet creation date.

The base of an aezeed phrase is 19 bytes long: 1 byte of version (letting the wallet know how to re-derive the addresses), 2 bytes of timestamp expressed in days since Bitcoin's genesis block (which covers dates up to 2188), and 16 bytes of entropy used to derive the seed. The timestamp tells the software the date from which to scan the blockchain.

These 19 bytes are encrypted with AEZ, an AEAD scheme resistant to nonce reuse, using a key derived by Scrypt (`n=32768`, `r=8`, `p=1`) from an optional passphrase (default: the string “aezeed”). The 33-byte ciphertext is encoded as 24 words using the same word list as BIP-0039. Unlike BIP-0039, the mnemonic phrase is itself a ciphertext, so the passphrase can be changed, or the encryption parameters updated, without generating new keys.
