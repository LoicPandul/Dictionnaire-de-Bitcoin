Mechanism that makes it possible to send a Lightning payment to a recipient who is not online at the time of sending. On the regular Bitcoin network, transactions are naturally asynchronous, since the recipient does not need to be connected to receive funds. The Lightning protocol, however, normally requires the receiver to be online to reveal the secret (the preimage) that finalizes the payment via an HTLC.

This constraint poses a significant problem for mobile wallets, which are not permanently connected. Several approaches have been proposed to resolve this limitation. The one described by the proposed BOLT-12 extension (pull request 1149 of the BOLT repository) and implemented by LDK relies on static invoices: an always-online node, for example the wallet provider's, serves an invoice on behalf of the offline recipient, and the sender's node (its LSP) holds the HTLC until the recipient reconnects. PTLCs (Point Time-Locked Contracts) have been considered to reduce the trust placed in the invoice server. Another approach, Eclair's, uses a trampoline relay node that holds the payment until the recipient reconnects, then forwards it to them.

Concrete implementations have appeared, notably in LDK and Eclair. LDK has integrated asynchronous payment logic on both the client side and the server side, while Eclair can wake up a disconnected mobile peer to pass it a pending payment. This work targets mobile devices, where permanent connectivity cannot be guaranteed.
