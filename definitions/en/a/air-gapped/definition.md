Describes a computing device that is physically isolated from any network, including the Internet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or any other wired or wireless connection. In the context of Bitcoin, an air-gapped device is used to secure private keys by keeping them away from any online attack vector.

The principle is simple: if a device is never connected to a network, it becomes extremely difficult for an attacker to reach it remotely and extract sensitive information. An air-gapped hardware wallet signs transactions offline. Data is exchanged with the computer connected to the Internet by physical means, typically a microSD card or a QR code, without ever establishing a direct connection.

This approach is one of the highest levels of security for storing bitcoin. Devices such as the Coldcard, the Foundation Devices Passport, or the Blockstream Jade offer this mode of operation.
