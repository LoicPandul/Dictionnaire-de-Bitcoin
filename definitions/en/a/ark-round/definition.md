Periodic process at the heart of the Ark protocol in which the Ark Service Provider (ASP) consolidates users' VTXO transactions and anchors them on the Bitcoin blockchain. Each round produces an on-chain Ark transaction containing a covenant tree in which all the output VTXOs created during that cycle are recorded.

Conceptually, the operation resembles a classic Bitcoin transaction: input VTXOs are spent and new output VTXOs are created. However, the on-chain liquidity for the outputs is advanced by the ASP. In return, users surrender their old VTXOs through forfeit transactions. The ASP recovers this liquidity later, when the input VTXOs expire. In the original Ark protocol, atomicity between the surrender of the old VTXOs and the creation of the new ones is guaranteed by connectors. Since version 0.1.0-beta.6, bark guarantees it differently, with hash locks (hArk protocol).

![In the original Ark protocol, during a round, the ASP advances 100,000 sats in the round transaction, which creates Alice's new VTXO and a connector; the forfeit transaction that surrenders the old VTXO to the ASP spends this connector and is therefore valid only if the round transaction is confirmed.](../../../fr/r/round-ark/assets/image-1.png)

Rounds take place at regular intervals, typically every 15 to 60 minutes depending on the ASP's configuration. For payments that need immediate settlement, users can turn to OOR (out-of-round) payments, which bypass this delay at the cost of a different trust model.
