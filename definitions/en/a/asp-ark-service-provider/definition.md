Operator of the Ark protocol, responsible for managing VTXOs and for periodically anchoring transactions on the Bitcoin blockchain. The ASP organizes "rounds" (at regular intervals, a parameter the operator configures; once an hour on Second's server in 2026) in which users' VTXO transactions are consolidated and recorded on-chain, which prevents any fractional reserve.

The ASP is non-custodial: it does not directly hold users' funds and therefore cannot steal them. However, if a user does not connect during the expiry period of their VTXOs (28 days on Second's server in 2026, a duration the operator configures), the ASP can recover the associated liquidity. This model steers Ark toward everyday payments rather than long-term storage. The ASP also provides interoperability with the Lightning Network through gateways, which lets Ark users send and receive Lightning payments.

ASPs are by nature larger than Lightning Service Providers (LSPs), which could favor a certain centralization of the system. To mitigate this risk, ASPs can be federated or operate peer-to-peer. For institutions or large bitcoin holders, operating an ASP is an opportunity to earn a yield with minimal trust required, by providing the liquidity the protocol needs.
