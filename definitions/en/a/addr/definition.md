Network message used on Bitcoin to communicate the addresses of nodes that accept inbound connections. This format, limited to 128 bits per address, was only suited to IPv6, IPv4, and Tor version 2 addresses. With the arrival of new protocols such as Tor v3 and the need for better scalability for future network protocols, the `addrv2` format was introduced in BIP-0155 to complement it. A node sends `addrv2` only to peers that signaled `sendaddrv2` during the handshake; Bitcoin Core keeps sending `addr` to the others.
