Political philosophy founded by Samuel Edward Konkin III in the 1970s. It is an extension of libertarianism that emphasizes direct action to break free from the yoke of state authority through counter-economics, an economic practice that takes place outside the frameworks regulated by the government. Agorist ideology rests on natural law, which holds that the natural rights of individuals are superior to the laws imposed by the state. This includes the primacy of private property, respect for physical integrity, and freedom of contract. Agorists reject every form of traditional political participation, such as voting, which they regard as a validation of the coercive authority of the state. They aspire to a society where economic and social exchanges take place freely in an open market, called the Agora, and aim at a peaceful revolution that gradually erodes the power of the state. Their emblem is “A3”, for “Agora, Anarchy, Action”. The principle of agorism is described in the *New Libertarian Manifesto*, published in 1980. This paper builds on Murray Rothbard's *For a New Liberty: The Libertarian Manifesto*, but goes even further. Many bitcoiners identify with agorism and believe that Bitcoin is its perfect tool.
