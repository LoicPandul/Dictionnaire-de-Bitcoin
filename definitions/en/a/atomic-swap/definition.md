Technology that lets two parties exchange cryptocurrencies directly, without trust and without an intermediary. These swaps are called “atomic” because they can only have two outcomes:
* Either the swap succeeds and both participants have exchanged their cryptocurrencies;
* Or the swap fails and both participants walk away with the cryptocurrencies they started with.

Atomic swaps can be carried out either with the same cryptocurrency, in which case they are also called “coinswaps”, or between different cryptocurrencies. Historically, they relied on hashed time-locked contracts (HTLCs), a timelock-based mechanism that guarantees the swap either completes or is canceled entirely, preserving the integrity of the funds of the parties involved. This method required protocols able to handle both scripts and timelocks. In recent years, however, the trend has shifted toward adaptor signatures. This second approach has the advantage of dispensing with scripts, which reduces operating costs. Its other major advantage is that it does not require the same hash on both legs of the transaction, which avoids revealing a link between them.
