Protocol proposed by David Chaum in 1982 and one of the first digital currency systems designed to preserve users' anonymity. It relies on public-key cryptography to create a digital currency that can be exchanged securely and anonymously. eCash works by creating digital tokens signed by a bank. It is therefore an evolution of deposit banks, though not a decentralized one. In transactions, these tokens are transferred between parties without revealing the users' identities, thereby preserving their privacy. eCash is considered a precursor of cryptocurrencies. It often comes up in discussions around Bitcoin, with some wanting to use eCash-like systems as a layer on top of it. Today, the trend is rather toward so-called “federated Chaumian” systems such as Fedimint.
