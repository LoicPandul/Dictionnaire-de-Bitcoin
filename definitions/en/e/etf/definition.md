Acronym for *Exchange-Traded Fund*: an investment fund listed on a stock exchange that tracks the performance of an asset or a basket of assets. Unlike a traditional fund, an ETF is bought and sold continuously on stock markets, like an ordinary share.

In the context of Bitcoin, spot Bitcoin ETFs, approved in the United States in January 2024, let institutional and retail investors gain exposure to the bitcoin price without holding the asset directly. The fund buys and holds actual bitcoin on behalf of its shareholders. There are also futures ETFs, which are based on derivative contracts rather than on physical holdings.

Bitcoin ETFs involve a trusted intermediary (the fund manager) and fees, and they do not confer the individual sovereignty that direct holding in self-custody provides.
