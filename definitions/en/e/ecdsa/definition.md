Acronym for *Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm*. Digital signature algorithm based on elliptic curve cryptography (ECC). It is a variant of the DSA algorithm (Digital Signature Algorithm). ECDSA uses the properties of elliptic curves to provide security levels comparable to those of traditional public-key algorithms such as RSA, while using much smaller keys. ECDSA covers the generation of key pairs (public key and private key) as well as the creation and verification of digital signatures.

In the context of Bitcoin, the public key is obtained from the private key by a point multiplication on the curve, independently of ECDSA. ECDSA is used to sign transactions, in order to satisfy a script to unlock bitcoin and spend it. The elliptic curve used on Bitcoin within ECDSA is `secp256k1`, defined by the equation $y^2 = x^3 + 7$. This algorithm has been used since the early days of Bitcoin in 2009. Today, it shares its place with Schnorr signatures, another digital signature scheme introduced with Taproot in 2021.
