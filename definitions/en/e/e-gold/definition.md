Gold-backed digital money system founded in 1996 by Douglas Jackson and Barry Downey. Users could open accounts denominated in grams of precious metals (gold, silver, platinum, palladium) stored in vaults. Transfers between accounts were instant and irreversible.

At its peak in 2006, e-gold processed more than $2 billion in transactions per year and had about 3.5 million accounts. The system attracted both legitimate users and illicit activity, because it had no identity verification.

In 2007, the US government indicted e-gold's operators for running an unlicensed money transmitting business and for money laundering. The company pleaded guilty in 2008. The shutdown of e-gold illustrated the vulnerability of centralized digital money systems to state action, a lesson that influenced the decentralized design of Bitcoin.
