Proposal that aims to increase Bitcoin's transaction capacity by adding a secondary block attached to the main block through a commitment in the coinbase transaction. The concept was first proposed by Johnson Lau in 2013 under the name *auxiliary blocks*, then taken up in March 2017 by Christopher Jeffrey, Joseph Poon, Fedor Indutny, and Stephen Pair, in the midst of the block size war. In this mechanism, a miner includes in the coinbase an `OP_RETURN` output that contains the Merkle root of an extension block. This secondary block has its own UTXO set and its own consensus rules, while still being validated by the full nodes that support it. Funds move between the main chain and the extension block through “resolution transactions” that consolidate the inputs and outputs between the two sets.

This proposal was never formalized as a BIP and was never activated. It was criticized for its complexity, for having effects similar to those of a hard fork from an economic standpoint, and for the lack of real benefits over SegWit.
