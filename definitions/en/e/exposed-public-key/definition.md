Public key known to others than its owner, because it can be read on the blockchain or deduced from an xpub that has circulated, while the funds it controls have not yet been spent. Against a quantum computer, this situation is the most exposed in the short term: Shor's algorithm recovers the private key from the public key, and only a known public key can serve as a starting point. Without a known public key, Shor's algorithm has nothing to compute: a hash would first have to be inverted, which remains out of reach.

A public key becomes visible in several ways. The first is the output script: P2PK and P2MS directly contain the public keys that can spend them, and P2TR contains the Taproot output key, that is, the internal key tweaked by a commitment to the script tree. In these three cases, the key is exposed as soon as the funds arrive. The other output types, P2PKH, P2WPKH, P2SH, P2WSH, and P2MR (proposed by BIP-0360, not yet activated), contain only a hash: as long as the output has not been spent and its address has not already been used, nobody sees the key. Their protection therefore rests on that hash, not on the strength of the curve.

The second way is spending. It generally reveals the public keys used, in the unlocking data of the transaction: the witness for SegWit and Taproot outputs, the scriptSig for P2PKH and P2SH. Let's take an example. Alice receives 2 payments on the same P2WPKH address, then spends one of them: her public key appears in the transaction, then stays in the blockchain forever. The second payment, still waiting on the same address, is now protected by an exposed key. This is the problem of address reuse, which turns an output exposed only at the time of spending into a durably exposed output. Spends on the 2017 forks, Bitcoin Cash and Bitcoin Gold, have a similar effect: since the UTXOs were duplicated, spending the twin coins on a fork revealed the public key of outputs still intact on Bitcoin.

The third way is sharing an extended public key (xpub). An xpub, which many exchanges, wallet providers, and payment services ask their customers to provide so they can generate receiving addresses, contains a public key and a chain code. With these two elements, anyone can already compute all the non-hardened child public keys; a quantum computer, for its part, could recover the private key associated with the xpub, then deduce the private keys of all these children. Only the keys located above the xpub in the tree, obtained by hardened derivation, remain out of reach: HMAC-SHA512 cannot be reversed. The hardened children of the xpub itself, however, are computed from its private key. BIP-0360 lists xpubs and wallet descriptors among the sources of public key exposure.

The volume involved is high, but hard to measure. According to BIP-0361, as of March 1, 2026, more than 34% of bitcoin sits in outputs whose public key has already been revealed on-chain; the initial version of the proposal, published on the bitcoindev list on July 12, 2025, said about 25%. The May 2025 Chaincode Labs report cites estimates ranging from a quarter of the supply, a little over 4 million BTC, to about half, almost 10 million BTC, and 6.26 million BTC according to Project Eleven. It also counts P2PK outputs, which make up about 0.025% of UTXOs but more than 1.7 million BTC, including the dormant coins of Satoshi's era.

A durably exposed key opens the door to the long-exposure attack. A key that appears only during spending falls under the short-exposure attack. BIP-0360 notes that protection against long exposure requires users not to expose their public keys, through key reuse or other risky practices, and proposes P2MR, an output type with no key path, whose script contains only the root of the script tree. Once exposed, a key can no longer be hidden: funds must move to a different output, and several proposals for post-quantum migration, including BIP-0361, deal precisely with the coins whose owners will not do so.

*BIP-0360 and BIP-0361 use the term "exposed public key"; BIP-0361 also says that a key has been "revealed on-chain".*
