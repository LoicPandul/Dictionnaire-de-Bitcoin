Cryptographer and cypherpunk, inventor of Hashcash and cofounder of Blockstream. On March 28, 1997, he announced on the cypherpunks mailing list an implementation of Hashcash, a postage system based on a partial hash collision: the sender of an email computes a token that is costly to produce, and the recipient verifies it instantly. This proof of work was aimed first at curbing spam and the abuse of anonymous remailers.

In 2008, Satoshi Nakamoto drew on it for Bitcoin. The white paper cites “Adam Back's Hashcash” as reference [6], for the proof of work that serves as a distributed timestamp, and on August 22, 2008, Satoshi wrote to Wei Dai that Back had pointed him to his paper, b-money. Bitcoin's proof of work thus builds on the principle of Hashcash.

On October 22, 2014, he co-signed, with Matt Corallo, Luke Dashjr, Mark Friedenbach, Gregory Maxwell, Andrew Miller, Andrew Poelstra, Jorge Timón, and Pieter Wuille, the paper “Enabling Blockchain Innovations with Pegged Sidechains,” which proposes sidechains with a two-way peg. The same year, he cofounded Blockstream with Pieter Wuille and others, the company behind the Liquid Network, and he leads it as chief executive officer according to the company's presentation page in 2026.
