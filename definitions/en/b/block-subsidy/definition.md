Amount of new units that the miner who solves a block can create. This subsidy is part of the block reward, together with transaction fees. It is distributed within a specific transaction called the coinbase. Initially set at 50 bitcoin per block in 2009, the subsidy is cut in half every 210,000 blocks (roughly every four years) through a process known as the halving. When the subsidy drops below 1 sat, it can no longer be collected, and the block reward will rely solely on transaction fees. Unless the protocol is modified, the money supply in circulation can then no longer be increased.
