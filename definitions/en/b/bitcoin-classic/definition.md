Fork proposal for the protocol launched in January 2016 after the abandonment of Bitcoin XT. It aimed to raise the block size limit from 1 MB to 2 MB with BIP-0109, an approach considered more moderate than Bitcoin XT, which proposed a much larger increase (8 MB, doubling every 2 years). The initiative was led by developers Gavin Andresen and Jeff Garzik, and had the backing of major ecosystem companies such as Coinbase. Bitcoin Classic emerged in a context of sharply intensifying Blocksize War and was classed in the big blockers camp. Although Bitcoin Classic attracted the attention and support of many players in the industry, its activation mechanism, which required 75% miner support, was controversial and ultimately contributed to its failure.
