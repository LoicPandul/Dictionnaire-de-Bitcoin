Cryptographic ring signature protocol that lets a signer prove that they hold a private key among a set of public keys, without revealing which one. The name refers to Borromean rings, a topological structure in which three rings are linked so that removing any one of them frees the other two. The scheme was formalized by Gregory Maxwell and Andrew Poelstra in 2015.

In the context of Bitcoin, Borromean ring signatures are used on the Liquid Network as the rangeproofs mechanism within Confidential Transactions. They prove that a committed amount lies within a valid range, without revealing its value. They are still in use there in 2026: Elements, the software behind Liquid, verifies amounts with these proofs, while the more compact Bulletproofs are only an alternative described in its documentation.
