Bitcoin address to which funds are sent irreversibly, with no possibility of spending them afterward. Nobody holds the corresponding private key: sending bitcoin to such an address amounts to destroying it for good, unless it becomes possible one day to break the underlying cryptography.

These addresses are generally constructed arbitrarily, without being derived from a private key, so that it can be proven that nobody knows it. The probability that a valid private key corresponds to a randomly chosen address is astronomically low, which makes the funds effectively unrecoverable. One example is the address `1BitcoinEaterAddressDontSendf59kuE`.

Burning bitcoin can serve different purposes. The Counterparty protocol used it in 2014 during its proof of burn phase to distribute its own tokens in exchange for destroyed bitcoin. It can also be proof of an irreversible commitment in certain cryptographic mechanisms, or simply a playful act.
