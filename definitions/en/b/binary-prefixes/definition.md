Units used in computing to quantify multiples of data sizes based on powers of 2. Unlike metric system prefixes, which use base 10, binary prefixes such as kibi (Ki), mebi (Mi), gibi (Gi), and tebi (Ti) multiply by powers of 2 ($2^{10}$, $2^{20}$, $2^{30}$, $2^{40}$ respectively). These prefixes are inherited from the earliest ways of measuring the size of information on computers. They are sometimes found in Bitcoin, for example to designate the size limit of the `blocks/blk*.dat` files that store the raw blockchain data in the Bitcoin Core software. These files thus have a maximum capacity of 128 mebibytes (MiB), which is equivalent to a little over 134 megabytes (MB).
