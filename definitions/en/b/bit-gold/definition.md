Decentralized digital money system conceived by Nick Szabo in 1998 and publicly described in 2005. Bit gold was designed to generate and exchange a virtual resource called bit gold. The system did not rely on any physical good, but aimed to create a form of unforgeable scarcity. The bit gold protocol relied on money creation through proof of work, where pieces of bit gold were created with the computing power of computers, forming a chain of proofs of work. Each proof of work was timestamped and then added to an ownership registry. Verification and transfer of ownership of bit gold were carried out through a public registry, in which users were identified by public keys. Bit gold remained a concept and was never implemented. This system is one of the precursors of Bitcoin along with b-money and RPoW, but Satoshi seemed to be unaware of its existence before creating Bitcoin. Satoshi mentioned it later on the Bitcoin website and forum.
