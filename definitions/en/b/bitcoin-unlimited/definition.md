Proposal for an update to the Bitcoin protocol that appeared at the end of 2015, aiming to increase the block size flexibly through a hard fork. The fork was backed by big blockers, notably Roger Ver, during the Blocksize War. Unlike Bitcoin Classic, Bitcoin Unlimited set no upper limit on block size, but let users define their own parameters. These parameters included the maximum generated block size, the excessive block size, and the excessive acceptance depth. However, Bitcoin Unlimited was criticized for its technical complexity and its security flaws. The update was never adopted.
