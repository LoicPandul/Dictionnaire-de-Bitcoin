Protocol proposed by Justus Ranvier in 2015 that aims to solve the critical problem of Bitcoin address reuse, a practice that seriously compromises users' privacy on the system. In the Bitcoin white paper, Satoshi Nakamoto had already stressed the importance of using a distinct key pair for each transaction in order to keep the different actions of users separate. BIP-0047 introduces the concept of reusable payment codes, which let a user receive multiple payments without having to manually generate a new Bitcoin address for each transaction. These codes act as virtual identifiers, derived from the seed of the user's wallet and located at the account level in the derivation structure of an HD wallet. From the combination of the payment codes of the two parties, each can derive a large number of unique addresses belonging to the other party, without needing to communicate with it again. The core of this protocol is the Elliptic-Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH) algorithm, a variant of the Diffie-Hellman key exchange built on elliptic curves, which lets the two parties establish a shared secret for generating unique receiving addresses. Although BIP-0047 was not added to Bitcoin Core and received a mixed reception from the community, implementations such as PayNym in Samourai Wallet (until its shutdown in April 2024) and Sparrow Wallet adopted it and fully integrated it into their ecosystem of privacy tools.
