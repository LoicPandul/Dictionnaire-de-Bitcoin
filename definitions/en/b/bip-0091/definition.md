Proposal by James Hilliard to ease the activation of the SegWit soft fork, defined in BIP-0141, BIP-0143, and BIP-0147, through a MASF without directly reaching the required threshold of 95% of hash power signaling support via bit 1. BIP-0091 lets miners signal their support for SegWit indirectly by using bit 4 in the blocks they mine. Once 269 blocks out of a window of 336 blocks have included bit 4 (that is, 80% of the hash power), BIP-0091 locks in, after which all compatible nodes must reject blocks that do not include bit 1. This approach aimed to make BIP-0148 (UASF) obsolete and to avoid a potential chain split on August 1, 2017. BIP-0091 was finally activated on July 23, 2017 (at block 477,120), just before the deadline of August 1 imposed by BIP-0148. This forced miners to signal SegWit, which was finally locked in on August 9 at block 479,808, then activated on August 24 at block 481,824. To sum up, BIP-0148 (UASF) was created in response to miners not signaling SegWit enough, but was never ultimately enforced. BIP-0091 (MASF) was created in response to BIP-0148 in order to force miners' hand without risking the UASF of BIP-0148. BIP-0091 is itself a soft fork, which ultimately forces miners to lock in the SegWit soft fork through the baseline method (MASF of BIP-0009).
