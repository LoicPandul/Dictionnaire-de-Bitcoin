Proposal for a decentralized Bitcoin mining pool, designed by Bob McElrath. Braidpool aims to solve the centralization problem of traditional mining pools, where the pool operator selects the transactions and controls the construction of blocks.

The protocol relies on a DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph) mined in parallel with the Bitcoin blockchain. The blocks of the DAG are “weak blocks”: valid Bitcoin blocks that do not meet the difficulty target of the main network, but only a lower target specific to Braidpool. These weak blocks play the role of shares and make it possible to measure each miner's contribution. Unlike P2Pool, which uses a linear sharechain, the DAG structure tolerates network latency better and reduces the stale block rate.

Each miner builds their own block templates and chooses the transactions to include, which preserves the decentralization of Bitcoin consensus. At the end of each difficulty adjustment period, rewards are distributed to miners through a multisig transaction that uses the FROST protocol. As of October 2026, Braidpool is still under development: its roadmap is at its first releases (data collection and test network), with no production deployment.
