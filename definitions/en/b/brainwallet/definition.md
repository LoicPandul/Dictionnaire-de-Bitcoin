Method of backing up a Bitcoin wallet that consists of keeping the mnemonic phrase on no physical or digital medium and memorizing it only. By extension, the term sometimes also covers choosing the words of one's mnemonic phrase oneself (instead of generating them randomly) in order to memorize it more easily. In both cases, the goal is to depend on no physical medium. Historically, the term first refers to a process that predates mnemonic phrases: the private key is derived directly from a passphrase chosen by the user, as the online generator brainwallet.org did in its 2013 version.

This approach is dangerous in two ways. First, human memory is fallible: forgetting, an accident, or a death leads to a permanent loss of funds, and raises an obvious problem of succession and inheritance. Second, when the user picks the words of the mnemonic phrase themselves, the entropy of the phrase is dramatically low. Humans are unable to produce true randomness: even a phrase that looks random follows predictable patterns (common words, quotes, song lyrics...). Attackers exploit these biases with dictionaries and brute-force techniques, and many brainwallets have been emptied within seconds of their creation.

The only scenario in which a brainwallet can be justified is an urgent escape (border crossing, checkpoint...), where no physical medium can be carried without risk. Even then, less risky alternatives exist: physically hiding the phrase, steganography, encryption...
