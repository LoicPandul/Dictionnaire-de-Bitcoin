Bitcoin fork launched in 2015 by the well-known developer Mike Hearn and backed by Gavin Andresen (former lead maintainer of Bitcoin after Satoshi's departure). Bitcoin XT was originally an implementation of the Bitcoin protocol compatible with Bitcoin Core. However, in August 2015, version 0.11A of Bitcoin XT adopted BIP-0101: a hard fork proposed to raise the block size limit from 1 MB to 8 MB, with a planned increase doubling that size every two years until it reached just over 8 GB per block in 2036. This proposal was one of the *casus belli* of the Blocksize War that took place between 2015 and 2017. It was backed by influential companies such as BitPay, Blockchain.info, and Circle, but very few miners signaled for it: at the end of August 2015, a single pool, Slush, about 1% of the hashrate, supported it. In the end, Bitcoin XT failed to win enough support from the community, and Mike Hearn eventually announced his departure from the community and the sale of his bitcoin. He expressed his disappointment in a blog post in which he declared, among other things, that Bitcoin had failed.
