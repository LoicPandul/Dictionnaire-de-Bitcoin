Any behavior of a system that does not comply with its own specifications, producing nonconforming results, also known as Byzantine behavior.

“Natural Byzantine faults” are commonly distinguished from “intentional Byzantine faults”: the former generally stem from undetected physical errors (memory, network transmissions, etc.), while the latter mainly result from attacks aimed at making the system fail (sabotage, malice, backdoor).

Authentication, signatures, and consensus by cryptographic means, together with “Byzantine Fault Tolerant” (BFT) algorithms, make it possible to limit the latter.

*The Byzantine fault refers to the Byzantine Generals' Problem, a metaphor dealing with the questioning of the reliability of transmissions and the integrity of the parties communicating.*
