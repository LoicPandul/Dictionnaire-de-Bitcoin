Period of intense debate and conflict within the Bitcoin community between 2015 and 2017. The conflict was over whether the block size, limited to 1 megabyte since 2010 by Satoshi Nakamoto, should be increased so that Bitcoin could process more transactions per block and thereby scale.

The supporters of a larger block size (known as “big blockers”), led by early developers such as Jeff Garzik, Gavin Andresen, and Mike Hearn, influential figures of the ecosystem such as Roger Ver, and a good share of the large mining companies, argued that the increase was necessary to reduce transaction fees and improve Bitcoin's scalability. They believed the change could be made without any consequence for the security of the system. On the other side, the opponents of a larger block size (known as “small blockers”), mainly Bitcoin Core developers (Pieter Wuille, Peter Todd, Gregory Maxwell, Wladimir van der Laan, Luke Dashjr...) and part of the technical community, argued that the change would centralize the system because of the higher operating load on nodes. They put forward so-called second-layer scaling solutions, such as the Lightning Network, which could handle a large number of transactions without changing the base protocol.

The debate over Bitcoin's approach to scalability goes back at least to 2010, with the first attempt to increase the block size in October of that year through [a patch proposed by Jeff Garzik](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1347.0). But the conflicts were at their most virulent during the Blocksize War of 2015 to 2017, with several fork attempts (Bitcoin XT, Bitcoin Classic, Bitcoin Unlimited, SegWit, SegWit2X, and Bitcoin Cash), propaganda and influence campaigns, and personal attacks. The abandonment of the SegWit2X hard fork on November 8, 2017, following massive opposition from the community (“NO2X”), is often seen as the event that ended the Blocksize War, while the casus belli is sometimes considered to be version 0.11A of the Bitcoin XT client, which implemented BIP-0101 on August 15, 2015. The end of the Blocksize War could also be placed on August 24, 2017, the date on which the SegWit soft fork was activated following the “UASF” campaign.
