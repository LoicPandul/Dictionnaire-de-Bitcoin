Acronym for *User-Activated Soft Fork*. Soft fork on Bitcoin initiated and enforced by the users of the network through their nodes, without depending on the approval of miners. Network nodes update their software to adopt the new rules of the soft fork, come what may.

Typically used in an emergency, notably when miners are mostly opposed to adopting a soft fork, the UASF is a means of pressure to avoid an excessive concentration of power among miners. In practice, the UASF has even become a deterrent, brandished by node operators when miners abuse their power. However, if a UASF is actually enforced, it carries risks, notably the possibility of a chain split, creating a new chain that may lack economic value and security. The first formal UASF proposal came from the developer Shaolin Fry, who pushed BIP-0148 in March 2017 to pressure miners who refused to signal for SegWit.
