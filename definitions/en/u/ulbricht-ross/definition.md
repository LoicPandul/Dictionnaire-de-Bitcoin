Founder of Silk Road, a dark web black market where payments are made in bitcoin, Ross William Ulbricht is one of the faces of the first legal cases tied to Bitcoin.

According to the press release of the US Attorney in New York, he created Silk Road in January 2011. The site operated until October 2013, when the authorities shut it down. Ulbricht was arrested in San Francisco on October 1, 2013: the prosecution identified him as "Dread Pirate Roberts", the site's administrator, which his defense disputes. On May 29, 2015, after a jury trial, the federal court for the Southern District of New York sentenced him to life in prison, with a forfeiture of $183,961,921. He was convicted notably of distributing narcotics over the Internet, engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, conspiracy to commit computer hacking, and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

On January 21, 2025, President Donald Trump pardoned him: the US Department of Justice's list of clemency actions carries that date.

The case makes Silk Road a textbook example in debates over the criminal use of Bitcoin, the traceability of transactions on the blockchain, and the proportionality of sentences.
