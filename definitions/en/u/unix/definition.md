Family of multitasking operating systems developed from 1969 by Bell Labs. Designed to be simple, flexible, and portable, UNIX has influenced many modern systems. Its core principles include a hierarchical file structure, the use of shell scripts, and modular utilities. UNIX is the origin of many variants, such as the BSD systems. Linux, released in 1991, is a Unix-like system: designed as a clone of UNIX, it was rewritten without reusing its code.
