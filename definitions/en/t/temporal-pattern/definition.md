Certain human behaviors are recognizable on-chain. The most useful one in chain analysis is perhaps the sleep rhythm. When a user is asleep, in principle, no Bitcoin transactions are broadcast. Yet people generally sleep at about the same times. Temporal analysis is therefore commonly used in chain analysis. It is quite simply the tally of the hours at which the transactions of a given entity are broadcast to the Bitcoin network. Analyzing these temporal patterns makes it possible to infer a good deal of information.

First, temporal analysis sometimes identifies the nature of the traced entity. If transactions are observed to be broadcast steadily over 24 hours, this betrays strong economic activity. The entity behind these transactions is probably a business, potentially international and perhaps with automated internal procedures. Conversely, if the temporal pattern is instead spread over 16 specific hours, the entity can be assumed to be an individual user, or perhaps a local business, depending on the volumes exchanged.

Beyond the nature of the observed entity, the temporal pattern can also indicate the user's approximate location through time zones. Other transactions can then be linked, and their timestamps used as an additional heuristic in a chain analysis.

In a different vein, a temporal analysis of this kind also made it possible to formulate the hypothesis that Satoshi Nakamoto was not operating from Japan but from the United States: [_The Time Zones of Satoshi Nakamoto_](https://medium.com/@insearchofsatoshi/the-time-zones-of-satoshi-nakamoto-aa40f035178f).
