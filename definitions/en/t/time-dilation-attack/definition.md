Attack on the Lightning Network that combines an eclipse attack with a deliberate slowing of block delivery to the victim. The attacker takes control of all the connections of the victim's Bitcoin node, then feeds it new blocks with a calculated delay (typically just under Bitcoin Core's 30-minute detection threshold). The victim gradually falls behind the real chain. This time lag is exploited to bypass the Lightning Network security mechanisms that rely on timelocks. For example, the attacker can broadcast an old, revoked commitment transaction on the real network. The victim, whose view of the blockchain is behind, does not detect this fraud attempt in time to publish a penalty transaction before the dispute period expires. The attack requires no mining power.

The concept was formalized in 2020 by Antoine Riard and Gleb Naumenko. Three variants have been identified, the most effective of which (the packet finalization attack) needs only a few hours of eclipse and a single channel with the victim. Countermeasures rely on using watchtowers on independent infrastructure, receiving blocks through alternative channels, and strengthening anti-eclipse protections.
