Concept from Austrian economics that describes the tendency of individuals to prefer immediate satisfaction to future satisfaction. An individual with a high time preference favors present consumption; an individual with a low time preference accepts postponing consumption for a greater benefit in the future. The concept is central to the Austrian theory of interest and capital.

Time preference is directly tied to the quality of the money in use. Money that loses value over time (through inflation) encourages a high time preference: it is rational to spend quickly rather than save. Conversely, hard money, whose value holds or appreciates, encourages saving, long-term planning, and productive investment.

Bitcoin, through its absolute scarcity and its disinflationary monetary policy, is often presented as a tool that favors a low time preference. It is said to push its holders toward a long-term outlook, both in their financial choices and in their consumption behavior.
