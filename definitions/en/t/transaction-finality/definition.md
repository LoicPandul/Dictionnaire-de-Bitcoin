Concept designating the moment from which a transaction is considered irreversible and permanently recorded in the blockchain. On Bitcoin, finality is probabilistic rather than absolute: each new block added on top of the block that contains the transaction makes reversing it exponentially harder and more expensive. By convention, a transaction is generally considered final after 6 confirmations, or about an hour, because the probability that an attacker could reorganize the chain, or that an natural fork could reach such a depth, is extremely low. However, for modest amounts, one or two confirmations may be judged sufficient.
