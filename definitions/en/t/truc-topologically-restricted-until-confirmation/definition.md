Acronym for *Topologically Restricted Until Confirmation*. Bitcoin Core relay policy that applies to version 3 transactions (`nVersion=3`) and limits their topology as long as they are unconfirmed: at most one unconfirmed ancestor and one unconfirmed descendant, of bounded size. These limits make transaction replacement (RBF) and fee bumping by a child transaction (CPFP) more reliable, notably for layer 2 protocols such as Lightning.

The sender opts in by setting the `nVersion` field of its transaction to 3. A node that applies the policy then adds the following rules to its usual standardness and mempool policy rules:

* an unconfirmed TRUC transaction has at most one unconfirmed ancestor and at most one unconfirmed descendant, and these linked transactions are themselves TRUC. A non-TRUC transaction cannot spend an unconfirmed output of a TRUC transaction, and vice versa. Outputs that are already confirmed can be spent without restriction;
* a TRUC transaction weighs at most 10,000 vB (size adjusted for sigops), and at most 1,000 vB (adjusted in the same way) if it depends on an unconfirmed TRUC parent;
* if a TRUC parent already has a child, a new child can evict the first one (sibling eviction) provided it complies with the replacement rules;
* a TRUC parent can be relayed below the minimum fee threshold, even with zero fees, provided it arrives with its child in a 1-parent-1-child package whose overall fee rate reaches that minimum (see PACKAGE RELAY). Since version 31.0, this exception also applies to a non-TRUC parent.

Let's take an example. Alice and Bob share a Lightning channel of the zero-fee commitment type, defined by BOLTs #1228. Their commitment transaction is signed in advance with a fee rate of 0, in version 3, and contains a P2A output that anyone can spend. When Alice closes the channel, she broadcasts this commitment with a child that spends the P2A output and pays the fees at the current rate. Bob would like to prevent confirmation. On a node that applies the policy, he cannot attach to the commitment a chain of descendants or a large child with a low fee rate, as in a pinning attack (see TRANSACTION PINNING): the commitment has only one child, which weighs at most 1,000 vB. If Bob placed his own child first, Alice's child can evict it.

This protection has limits. It is a relay rule rather than a consensus rule: it acts node by node, and BIP-0431 notes that the security of a transaction depends on the share of the network that adopts it. TRUC-type techniques, designed against pinning, also do not prevent the replacement cycling attack (Bitcoin Optech Newsletter of October 25, 2023). The principle is disputed: Peter Todd, who defends his own proposal of replacement by fee rate (replace-by-feerate), judges TRUC to be bad (Bitcoin Optech Newsletter of July 26, 2024), and BIP-0431 argues, on the contrary, against certain variants of that proposal. With the cluster mempool of Bitcoin Core 31.0, the mempool's ancestor and descendant limits disappear in favor of limits per group of linked transactions, and the CPFP carve-out is removed: the release notes say that contracts needing a similar function are expected to use TRUC and sibling eviction. The TRUC limits remain in the code.

On September 23, 2022, Gloria Zhao proposed these rules for "V3" transactions on the bitcoin-dev mailing list, in order to solve pinning through rule 3 of BIP-0125 and possibly remove the CPFP carve-out. The proposal became BIP-0431, added to the BIP repository on May 29, 2024. Bitcoin Core renamed "v3" to "TRUC" in its code on July 2, 2024. It made version 3 standard in version 28.0, released on October 2, 2024, and its wallet has been able to create and spend TRUC transactions since version 30.0. On the Lightning side, BOLTs #1228 was merged on May 4, 2026. As of October 24, 2026, BIP-0431 is an informational document with Draft status.

*TRUC transactions are also called "v3" transactions, the original name of the proposal. Not to be confused: TRUC is the relay policy, NVERSION is the transaction field that signals it, and BIP-0431 is the document that describes it.*
