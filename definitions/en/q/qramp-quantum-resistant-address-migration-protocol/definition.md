BIP draft written by Agustin Cruz and presented on the bitcoindev mailing list on February 11, 2025, that would impose a deadline for moving bitcoin held at classical addresses (protected by ECDSA) to quantum-resistant addresses. After this deadline, nodes would reject any spend of a classical output: the funds left behind would become permanently unspendable. The draft never received a BIP number.

The text, as web archives preserved it in April 2025, describes a two-phase hard fork. Before the deadline, classical addresses and quantum-resistant addresses are both valid, and the old script rules apply. After the deadline, the script interpreter compares the block height or time with the deadline: if the output being spent is locked by a classical address, script evaluation fails immediately. After the deadline, a transaction that mixes classical inputs and quantum-resistant inputs is rejected as a whole.

Let's take an example. Alice holds bitcoin at an address that starts with `1`, a type the text cites among classical addresses. If she sends it to a quantum-resistant address before the deadline, her bitcoin remains spendable after the date. If she lets the date pass, no up-to-date node will accept a transaction spending this output any longer: the bitcoin is neither burned nor transferred to someone else, it simply becomes impossible to spend. The text gives values as examples only (activation at block 680,000, deadline at block 700,000) and leaves the length of the migration period to be set after discussion. It defines neither an address format nor a signature scheme: XMSS, LMS, and lattice-based signatures are cited as examples, the choice being deferred to another document.

In his message of February 11, 2025, Agustin Cruz defends a firm deadline rather than a gradual, voluntary migration, and weighs the risk of permanently locked funds against that of an attack with catastrophic consequences. The text itself acknowledges permanent loss of funds among the criticisms, as well as the risk of a chain split: if only 60% of nodes are up to date, the others would keep accepting classical spends after the deadline. Cruz replies to Dustin Ray that freezing is a strict security measure, which has to be taken before the danger so that lost coins cannot be stolen and then put back into circulation, and he cites Satoshi's coins in this regard, should Satoshi no longer be alive.

The discussion focused mostly on the principle of making bitcoin unspendable, much more than on the text. Bitcoin Optech newsletter #348, dated April 4, 2025, notes it: very little of the discussion is about the draft itself. Dustin Ray judges that a pure freeze poses serious problems, since it would cancel a significant part of the money supply, and on February 19 proposes, as a hypothesis, a “turnstile”: after a given height, pre-quantum funds could no longer be spent normally, and a new transaction type would migrate them provided it proves knowledge of the secret that produced the public key (for P2PKH addresses only). Hunter Beast prefers to let those who have a quantum computer put the old coins back into circulation. Pieter Wuille replies the same day that “of course they have to be confiscated”: if a quantum computer able to break the cryptography becomes a credible threat, the ecosystem will have no choice but to disable, by soft fork, spending with the vulnerable schemes before the machine exists. He also raises a possibility, keeping the spend available to those who prove knowledge of an additional secret. Michal Kolesár judges any constraint of this kind bad. ArmchairCryptologist observes that a mandatory migration runs up against the block size limit: some actors could saturate the network with fake transactions to push fees up before the deadline.

Optech presents the draft as a project aimed at destroying vulnerable bitcoin. The text captured in April 2025 does not, however, speak of destruction, and Cruz writes on May 25, 2025 that QRAMP aims neither at confiscation nor at control. The same day, he describes another version of it, in three phases: optional post-quantum keys in Taproot outputs, then the announcement of a soft fork that would disable vulnerable scripts after a long grace period (about 4 years), and finally a gradual disabling of vulnerable outputs according to their age or inactivity, with possible exceptions on proof of ownership. No published document corresponds to this version.

The GitHub repository that hosted the text no longer exists, and only two identical captures from April 5 and 7, 2025 remain. No published revision has been found since, and Cruz has not announced an abandonment on the bitcoindev list, where his last message on the subject dates from May 25, 2025. The name is nonetheless still cited as a reference point in later threads: in August 2025, the QSAVE proposal presents itself as an alternative to QRAMP, which makes coins unspendable, and in January 2026 a message proposes a “quarantine mode” as an alternative to freezing classical signatures.

BIP-0361, published in the BIP repository in 2026, cites neither QRAMP nor Agustin Cruz, but presents a related idea: a deadline announced long in advance, after which classical outputs can no longer be spent normally. It differs in its two-phase soft fork and in an announced but undefined rescue protocol, which would let the true owner recover funds by proving a secret (except for P2PK outputs), whereas the QRAMP text captured in April 2025 offers no recovery path after the deadline. See quantum-safe rescue protocol for this mechanism, post-quantum migration for the full plan, and Satoshi's coins and Hourglass for the other responses to the fate of coins that will not migrate.

*The draft was written in February 2025 (the text is dated the 10th), but the Optech newsletter that summarizes it dates from April 2025: that is not the date of its publication.*
