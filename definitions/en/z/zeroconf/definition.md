Risky practice of treating a Bitcoin transaction as final, and carrying out the associated act in return (such as the sale of a good or a service), before the transaction has actually been included in a block on the blockchain. Unconfirmed transactions, or zeroconf transactions, are vulnerable to double-spend attacks, because they are not yet irrevocably recorded in the ledger. Zeroconf may possibly be considered in very specific contexts where speed takes priority, such as small commercial transactions or a transaction between close acquaintances. In these situations, the double-spend risk is often considered acceptable compared with the advantage of a fast transaction. Nevertheless, for large transactions, especially when the sender is unknown, it is crucial to wait for several confirmations before considering the transaction immutable. The generally accepted standard is to wait for 6 confirmations, which means that 5 additional blocks must be mined after the one including the transaction before it is considered final.
