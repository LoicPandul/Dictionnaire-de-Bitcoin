Computer scientist Philip R. Zimmermann, known as Phil Zimmermann, is the author of PGP (Pretty Good Privacy), a public-key email encryption program, and one of the figures in the 1990s debates over the export of cryptography.

In June 1991, PGP was released for free on the Internet. The software spread around the world, and US authorities concluded that the restrictions on exporting cryptography had been violated. The Customs Service opened a criminal investigation of Zimmermann that lasted three years. On January 11, 1996, the federal prosecutor's office for the Northern District of California (San Jose office) announced that the investigation had been dropped: Zimmermann was never indicted. He then founded PGP Inc.

PGP enters the history of Bitcoin through Hal Finney. In 2013, Finney recounted that he got his start in cryptography working on an early version of PGP, closely with Zimmermann, that he was one of the first hires when Zimmermann started his company, which he calls "PGP Corporation," and that he was active among the cypherpunks. In January 2009, he received the first Bitcoin transaction from Satoshi Nakamoto. The principle PGP made commonplace, public-key cryptography, is also the one behind Bitcoin's keys and signatures.
