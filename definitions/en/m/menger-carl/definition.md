Austrian economist, founder of the Austrian school, who explains the spontaneous emergence of money by the ease with which certain goods can be sold.

Born in 1840 in Galicia, then a province of the Austrian Empire, Carl Menger published the *Grundsätze der Volkswirthschaftslehre* (*Principles of Economics*) in 1871. In it he defends a subjective theory of value: a good is worth what it brings to the person who desires it, not the labor it cost. This work earned him a position at the University of Vienna, where he taught until 1903. In June 1892, he published in the *Economic Journal* the article "On the Origin of Money." Goods differ in how easily they can be sold, their saleability; in an exchange without money, everyone has an interest in swapping a less saleable good for a more saleable one, and money gradually narrows down to the most saleable goods, gold and silver in particular.

He died in 1921 and never knew Bitcoin. Texts on Bitcoin nonetheless invoke his theory, in both directions: in 2021, an article in *Bitcoin Magazine* uses it to explain the emergence of Bitcoin, whereas in 2014 Chris Leroux, starting from the 1892 article, uses it to argue that Bitcoin cannot become a money.
