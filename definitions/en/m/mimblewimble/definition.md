Protocol that aims to improve the privacy and scalability of transactions. The white paper for this protocol was posted in 2016 on the `#bitcoin-wizards` IRC channel by a pseudonymous user. Andrew Poelstra then formalized the protocol in a paper published in October 2016. Mimblewimble relies on Confidential Transactions and Pedersen commitments to hide amounts, without preventing verification that inputs equal outputs. It uses neither addresses nor a scripting system: authentication relies solely on the algebraic structure of the transactions. All the transactions in a block are aggregated into a single one, like a giant coinjoin, which makes the links between sender and recipient indistinguishable.

The protocol was implemented in two separate blockchains launched in January 2019: Grin and Beam. Litecoin integrated an adapted version in the form of extension blocks in May 2022. Mimblewimble has never been integrated into Bitcoin because it is incompatible with Bitcoin's scripting system and current UTXO model.
