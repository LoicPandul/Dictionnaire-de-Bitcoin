Variant of MuSig proposed in 2020 by Jonas Nick, Tim Ruffing, Yannick Seurin, and Pieter Wuille. The acronym “DN” stands for “Deterministic Nonces”. The protocol eliminates the nonce commitment round by requiring each signer to derive its nonces deterministically from its private key and the message, then to provide a zero-knowledge proof attesting that the computation is correct. This mechanism brings signing down to two rounds of communication while preventing nonce manipulation.

Despite its theoretical elegance, generating the proofs turns out to be too resource-intensive for practical use, notably on hardware wallets. MuSig-DN remained an intermediate research step and was superseded by MuSig2, which achieves the same two-round goal with a lighter approach based on multiple random nonces.
