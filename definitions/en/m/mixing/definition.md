Property of a dynamical system, in mathematics, whereby after a certain time all portions of the initial space can in theory end up mixed with any other portion. Mixing implies that the position of a particle or the state of a system evolves in such a way that its future distribution is independent of its initial distribution, reaching a state where the characteristics of the initial state are uniformly distributed throughout the space of the system. In the context of Bitcoin, this notion can be used to assess the quality of a coin mixing process such as a coinjoin.

*Some bitcoiners distinguish mixing from the coinjoin process. They say that mixing refers to coins being mixed by an entity that holds the funds, as opposed to coinjoins, where the user always keeps possession of the funds. In my view, however, this distinction is incorrect, because a coinjoin necessarily involves mixing in the mathematical sense of the term.*
