Concept that applies Darwin's theory of natural selection to the monetary domain. It postulates that the different forms of money compete with one another and that those with the best-suited properties (scarcity, durability, divisibility, portability, fungibility, verifiability) end up supplanting the less effective ones.

From this perspective, proponents of the concept see Bitcoin as a superior money likely to prevail over fiat currencies through the sole strength of its intrinsic properties. Its strictly limited supply, its censorship resistance, and its decentralized nature would be decisive competitive advantages in this monetary selection.

The concept is closely linked to hyperbitcoinization and follows in the line of Thiers' law (good money drives out bad), in opposition to Gresham's law (bad money drives out good).
