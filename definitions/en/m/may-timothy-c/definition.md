American engineer, formerly of Intel, Timothy C. May, known as Tim May, is one of the founders of the cypherpunk movement and the author of its founding text on online anonymity. He left Intel in 1986.

In mid-1988, he wrote "The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto," which he distributed to a few techno-anarchists at the Crypto '88 conference. The text announces that computers will allow two people to exchange messages, do business, and negotiate electronic contracts without ever knowing each other's legal identity, through untraceable exchanges on encrypted networks.

In 1992, he cofounded with Eric Hughes and John Gilmore the cypherpunks mailing list, whose server John Gilmore hosted. He read his manifesto at the founding meeting in September 1992, then published it on the list on November 22, 1992. The press that hailed his death in December 2018 recalled that several building blocks of Bitcoin were born in the exchanges on this list, and credited his writings with an influence on the creation of Bitcoin, through the idea of a cryptographic money independent of states. Hal Finney, the first recipient of a transaction from Satoshi Nakamoto, was one of the cypherpunks.

Tim May died at his home in Corralitos, California.
