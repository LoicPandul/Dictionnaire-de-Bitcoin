Bitcoin exchange founded in 2010 by Jed McCaleb, then acquired in 2011 by Mark Karpelès, who ran it from Tokyo, Japan. At its peak, Mt. Gox handled about 70% of the world's bitcoin transactions, making it by far the largest exchange. In February 2014, Mt. Gox suspended withdrawals and then declared bankruptcy after revealing the loss of about 850,000 bitcoin, 750,000 of which belonged to its customers. About 200,000 bitcoin were found in March 2014 in an old wallet. The rehabilitation plan, approved in November 2021, provides for creditors to be repaid in bitcoin; in October 2025, the deadline was pushed back to October 31, 2026. The name “Mt. Gox” is an acronym for “Magic: The Gathering Online eXchange”, because the site was originally designed as a marketplace for cards from that game. The collapse remains one of the most significant events in Bitcoin's history and highlighted the risks of keeping one's bitcoin on a centralized exchange.
