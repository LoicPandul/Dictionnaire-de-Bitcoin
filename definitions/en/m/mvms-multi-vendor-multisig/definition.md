Acronym for *Multi-Vendor Multisig*. Multisignature setup in which each key is stored on a signing device from a different manufacturer, so that no open spending path can be satisfied by the keys of a single manufacturer alone.

The principle is diversity of components. Take a 3-of-3 made of three devices from the same manufacturer: if a flaw in its firmware or random number generator makes the keys predictable or extractable, the attacker gets all three keys at once, and the setup no longer protects against that flaw. With the same 3-of-3 spread across three manufacturers, the same flaw exposes only one key out of three, and the security margin falls to that of a 2-of-2 until the affected device is replaced. To spend without authorization through a given path, the attacker must then exploit as many independent flaws as that path requires keys, three for a 3-of-3, two for a 2-of-3, at the same time on the same wallet.

The diversity is never total. The devices follow the same standards (BIP-0032, the secp256k1 curve), often rely on the same cryptographic libraries, and may share hardware components. An MVMS therefore reduces the risk of a flaw specific to one manufacturer, without removing the risk of a flaw common to all of them.

An n-of-n multisig protects better against theft, but it increases the risk of loss: losing a device and its backup is enough to lock the funds. To reconcile the two, an MVMS is combined with *Miniscript* and *timelocks*: the main path requires the full threshold, then recovery paths open after a given delay, with a lower threshold. For example, a 3-of-3 becomes a 2-of-3 after 6 months without spending, then a 1-of-3 after 12 months. Since the delay is relative, it is measured from the confirmation of the output. The `nSequence` field encodes only 16 bits of it (BIP-0068), which is 65,535 blocks at most, about 15 months. Spending the coins to a new address of the same wallet resets the counter, so the coins have to be refreshed periodically, which also checks that the devices still work. Liana, the software from Wizardsardine, can build this type of wallet, with signing devices that support Miniscript descriptors.

The main cost is privacy. Scripts of this type are uncommon and, in P2WSH, reveal all their keys and all their recovery paths when spent: the transactions are recognizable on the chain. Taproot reveals less, since only the branch used appears. Management is also more complex, with a backup of both the mnemonic phrases and the descriptor, and the purchase of several devices from different manufacturers.

The concept was described in September 2026 by Loïc Morel in an article on the Wizardsardine blog, which presents it as an answer to flaws specific to one manufacturer, rather than adding a passphrase or generating the seed with dice. The acronym MVMS is a convenient abbreviation that the article does not use itself.
