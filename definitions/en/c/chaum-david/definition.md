Cryptographer, inventor of mix networks and blind signatures, then founder of the company DigiCash, which commercialized eCash, one of the first anonymous electronic currencies. He earned his PhD in computer science from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1982, with a thesis titled "Computer Systems Established, Maintained and Trusted by Mutually Suspicious Groups."

In February 1981, he published "Untraceable Electronic Mail, Return Addresses, and Digital Pseudonyms" in *Communications of the ACM*. Alice encrypts her message in several layers, one per relay; each relay peels off its layer and shuffles the order of the messages it received before forwarding them, so that an observer can no longer link the sender to the recipient. The Sphinx format, which Lightning uses for its onion routing (BOLT-04), is a packet format designed for such mix networks.

At the CRYPTO '82 conference, he presented "Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments." Bob asks the bank to sign a blinded token, and the bank debits his account: it signs without seeing the token. Once the blinding is removed, the signature remains valid, and when the merchant presents the token, the bank verifies its own signature without being able to link it to Bob's withdrawal. Chaum is also behind the International Association for Cryptologic Research (IACR), which he proposed creating at CRYPTO '82.

DigiCash put eCash online with its own unit, the CyberBucks, then issued it under license through banks, before going bankrupt in 1998. The system remained centralized: the bank issues the tokens and checks every payment against double spending, the role of trusted "mint" that the Bitcoin white paper replaces with a public ledger and proof of work.

His blind signatures later returned to Bitcoin: in the Chaumian coinjoin formalized by ZeroLink in 2017, then in ecash mints backed by bitcoin such as Cashu, which reuses a variant of his blinding, and Fedimint, which spreads the signing among several guardians.
