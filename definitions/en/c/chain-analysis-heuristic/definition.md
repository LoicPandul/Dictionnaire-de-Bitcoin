Family of empirical methods used to trace the flows of bitcoin on the blockchain, based on characteristics observed in transactions. A heuristic is a practical approach to solving problems, often through approximate methods, but one that offers a solution good enough to reach a given goal. These heuristics yield fairly reliable results, but never absolute precision. In other words, chain analysis always involves a degree of likelihood in the conclusions it draws. For example, an analyst can estimate with more or less certainty that two addresses belong to the same entity, but total certainty will always be out of reach. The whole purpose of chain analysis lies precisely in combining various heuristics to minimize the risk of error. It is, in a way, an accumulation of evidence that brings the analyst closer to reality. In this framework, internal heuristics are distinguished from external heuristics.

Internal heuristics focus on characteristics specific to a single transaction. Their analysis includes elements such as UTXO amounts, the scripts used, versions, or locktimes. For example, the round payment heuristic identifies a transaction output as likely being a payment if its amount is a round number. These heuristics often make it possible to identify the change (the money returned to the same user) and therefore to keep tracing.

External heuristics, for their part, analyze similarities and characteristics beyond the transaction itself. They cover the whole environment of the transaction. For example, address reuse across several transactions is an external heuristic. The CIOH is another.
