Economic phenomenon described by the Irish-French economist Richard Cantillon in the 18th century, according to which the injection of new money into an economy does not spread uniformly. The first beneficiaries of this newly created money (banks, financial institutions, governments) can spend it before prices rise, while the last to receive it (wage earners, savers, populations furthest from the banking system) bear the price increase without having benefited from early access to this liquidity. This creates a hidden redistribution of wealth in favor of those close to the source of money issuance.

Bitcoin is sometimes presented as an answer to this effect: its money issuance is programmed, predictable, and governed by the protocol. The block subsidy, which declines over the course of the halvings, is distributed to miners in exchange for work, and no central authority can decide to arbitrarily create new units. There is therefore no privileged intermediary able to capture the value of money creation ahead of the rest of the network.
