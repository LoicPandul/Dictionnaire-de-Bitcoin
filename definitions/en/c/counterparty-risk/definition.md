Risk that a third party to whom funds have been entrusted, or with whom an agreement has been made, fails to meet its obligations, resulting in a financial loss. In the context of Bitcoin, this risk arises mainly when users deposit their bitcoin on centralized exchanges (CEXs) or with other custodians.

The Bitcoin protocol was designed to eliminate the need to trust a third party. When a user holds their own private keys, they face no counterparty risk on their bitcoin. On the other hand, as soon as they entrust the custody of their funds to an entity, they depend on that entity's solvency, honesty, and technical competence.

Bitcoin's history is marked by massive losses tied to this risk: the Mt. Gox hack in 2014, the QuadrigaCX bankruptcy in 2019, and the FTX collapse in 2022. The Bitcoin community therefore insists on the principle of self-custody: personally holding one's private keys remains the only way to eliminate counterparty risk completely.
