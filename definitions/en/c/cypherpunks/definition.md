Informal, international community of people promoting the use of cryptography as a means of ensuring individual freedoms on the Internet. Cypherpunks defend the fundamental right of the individual to protect their privacy, particularly amid growing state surveillance and the exploitation of data by private entities. Their name comes from the English words *cypher*, a variant spelling of cipher meaning secret code, and *punk*. It is modeled on the term cyberpunk, which designates a literary genre describing a dystopian future where cutting-edge technology mixes with the vice and crime of a decaying society.

The cypherpunk movement dates back to the early 1990s, when groups of cryptographers, programmers, and libertarians began discussing the political implications of cryptography during their first meetings in Silicon Valley. The leading figure in the community was Tim May, who had written the *Crypto Anarchist Manifesto* in 1988, a founding text of the movement. Key moments in cypherpunk history include the founding, in 1992, of the “Cypherpunks mailing list”, an email mailing list that hosted these discussions. The publication by Eric Hughes in 1993 of *A Cypherpunk's Manifesto*, a document setting out the goals and actions of cypherpunks, was also an important moment.

The idea of an electronic money that does not rest on a central entity, like Bitcoin, is rooted in cypherpunk philosophy. The creation of Bitcoin is often considered a major achievement of this vision, alongside PGP, Tor, and WikiLeaks.
