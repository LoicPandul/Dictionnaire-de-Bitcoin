Property of a system that makes it able to operate without any entity being able to prevent legitimate participants from using it. In the context of Bitcoin, censorship resistance means that no government, company, or individual can block a valid transaction or forbid a user from accessing the network.

This property rests on several fundamental characteristics of Bitcoin: the decentralization of the network (thousands of nodes spread around the world), the absence of a central authority able to filter transactions, and the proof-of-work mechanism, which makes the inclusion of transactions depend only on the fees offered by the sender.

Censorship resistance is considered one of Bitcoin's most important value propositions, particularly in authoritarian regimes where citizens may see their bank accounts frozen or their transactions blocked. It fundamentally sets Bitcoin apart from centralized payment systems and CBDC projects, where an authority can decide at any time to exclude users.
