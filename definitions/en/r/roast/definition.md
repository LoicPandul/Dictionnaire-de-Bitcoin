Asynchronous Schnorr threshold signature protocol, whose acronym stands for *Robust Asynchronous Schnorr Threshold signatures*. ROAST was proposed in 2022 by Tim Ruffing, Viktoria Ronge, Elliott Jin, Jonas Schneider-Bensch, and Dominique Schröder. It works as a wrapper around an existing threshold signature scheme, such as FROST, making it resistant to uncooperative participants and high network latency.

In a classic threshold scheme, a subset of signers must coordinate their responses to produce a valid signature. If some participants do not respond or try to disrupt the process, the signing session can get stuck. ROAST solves this problem by automatically restarting parallel signing sessions until a functioning quorum is reached. This protocol is particularly useful for federations, such as the Liquid Network federation, where it could considerably increase the number of signers while keeping the signing process reliable.
