Lightning routing technique designed to protect the identity of a payment's recipient. In classic routing, the sender builds the entire route and therefore knows the final node. With rendez-vous routing, the recipient pre-builds an encrypted route segment from an intermediate node (the rendez-vous point) to itself, and passes this segment to the sender through the invoice. The sender completes the route on its side up to the rendez-vous point, without ever learning the nodes beyond it.

This approach has limitations, however: the sender cannot insert its own data into the portion encrypted by the recipient, and amounts must be fixed in advance in the partial onion, which complicates multi-party payments. For these reasons, rendez-vous routing was superseded by blinded paths, a more flexible technique that lets the sender freely choose the data inserted at each hop and that can be reused with onion messages.
