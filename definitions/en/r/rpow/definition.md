Acronym for *Reusable Proofs Of Work*. It is an electronic money system based on the transfer of tokens built on proofs of work, developed and implemented by Hal Finney in 2004. RPoW positioned itself as an improvement on the theoretical concepts of b-money and bit gold.

Unlike those two, RPoW did come into existence and was launched. RPoW could have taken the place that Bitcoin now holds. It was the most advanced electronic money project before Satoshi's invention. However, Bitcoin surpasses RPoW by solving two critical problems. First, Bitcoin introduced an automatic adjustment of mining difficulty, a mechanism absent from RPoW, which avoids the inflation caused by growing mining capacity and a growing number of miners. Second, whereas RPoW depended on central servers, Bitcoin established a distributed consensus mechanism. This mechanism rests on the principle that nodes synchronize on the chain with the most accumulated work, which eliminates the need for known servers. RPoW never received the support it needed to emerge and be adopted by the general public.

Unlike b-money and bit gold, RPoW was never cited by Satoshi Nakamoto, even though this system was probably the closest to his invention.
