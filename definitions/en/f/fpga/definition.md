Acronym for *Field-Programmable Gate Array*. Integrated circuit that can be reconfigured after manufacturing to perform specific operations. Unlike conventional processors (CPUs), which are designed to be general-purpose, FPGAs let developers program the hardware logic directly to fit a precise task, which makes them more efficient in power consumption and performance for that task.

In the history of Bitcoin mining, FPGAs were an intermediate step between GPU mining and the arrival of ASICs. Starting in 2011, miners began using FPGAs to compute `SHA256` hashes, because they offered a better performance-to-power ratio than GPUs while remaining reprogrammable. The adoption of FPGAs was nevertheless relatively short-lived, because ASICs, circuits entirely dedicated to `SHA256` hashing, quickly outperformed them in computing power and energy efficiency.
