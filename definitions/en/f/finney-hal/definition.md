Cryptographer and developer, known as Hal Finney (Harold T. Finney II), famous for his role in the early days of Bitcoin and his contributions to cryptography. From the publication of the Bitcoin white paper in 2008, he was one of the first to interact with Satoshi Nakamoto. He provided feedback, reported bugs, and proposed improvements after the software launched in January 2009. He was the recipient of the first Bitcoin transaction (apart from coinbase transactions), receiving 10 BTC from Satoshi in block 170:

```text
f4184fc596403b9d638783cf57adfe4c75c605f6356fbc91338530e9831e9e16
```

Hal Finney is also probably the first person, after Satoshi, to have mined a block: block 78. Beyond that, Hal Finney was the first promoter of Bitcoin during a period when the project was still little known.

Outside Bitcoin, he is also recognized for inventing RPoW (Reusable Proofs of Work), an electronic money system launched in 2004. Although RPoW did not meet the success expected, it remains one of the most accomplished precursors of Bitcoin. As a committed cypherpunk, Hal Finney also played a decisive role in the development and improvement of PGP (Pretty Good Privacy). Hal Finney died on August 28, 2014, of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, or Lou Gehrig's disease). He was cryonically preserved by the Alcor foundation. He will remain a major figure in the history of cryptography and Bitcoin.
