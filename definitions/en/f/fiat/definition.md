Currency, often issued by a state, whose status as legal tender is imposed by public force.

Its value is not based on any commodity such as gold or silver, but on the legal tender status that the state confers on it. The word comes from the Latin *fiat*, "let it be done," in the sense of an order or decree. Since the United States suspended the convertibility of the dollar into gold in 1971, the major national currencies are all of this type.
