Attack scenario in which miners try to rewrite a recently confirmed block in order to collect the transaction fees it contains, while also adding the high-fee transactions that have arrived in the mempool in the meantime. The ultimate goal of this attack for the miner is to increase profitability. Fee sniping can become more and more profitable as the block reward decreases and transaction fees make up a larger share of miners' revenue. It can also be advantageous when the fees in the previous block are far higher than those of the next best candidate block. To simplify, the miner faces this choice in terms of incentives:
* Mining normally on top of the last block, with a high probability of winning a small reward;
* Trying to mine an earlier block (fee sniping), with a low probability of winning a large reward.

![Facing the latest block N, the miner can either mine block N+1 on top of it, with a high chance of collecting small fees, or rewrite block N from block N−1 (fee sniping), with a low chance of collecting the fees of N plus those of the transactions that have arrived since.](./assets/image-1.png)

This attack is a risk to the Bitcoin system, because the more miners adopt it, the more the other, initially honest miners are incentivized to do the same. Each time a new miner joins those attempting fee sniping, the probability that one of the attacking miners succeeds rises, and the probability that one of the honest miners extends the chain falls accordingly. If this attack were carried out on a massive scale and sustained over time, block confirmations would no longer be a reliable indicator of the immutability of a Bitcoin transaction. This could potentially make the system unusable.

To counter this risk, most wallet software automatically fills in the `nLocktime` field so that the transaction is valid only if it is included at the next block height. This makes it impossible to include the transaction in a rewrite of the previous block. If the use of `nLocktime` becomes widespread among Bitcoin users, it considerably reduces the incentives for fee sniping. It encourages the blockchain to move forward rather than be rewritten, by reducing the potential benefits of rewriting. For Taproot transactions, BIP-0326 proposes using the `nSequence` field in a similar way to get an effect equivalent to that of the `nLocktime` field for other types of transactions. This use would achieve two things at once, as it would also improve the privacy of layer 2 protocols that use this same field.
