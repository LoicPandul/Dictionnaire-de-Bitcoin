Acronym for *Fast Internet Bitcoin Relay Engine*. Protocol designed by Matt Corallo in 2016 to speed up the propagation of Bitcoin blocks around the world. Its goal was to reduce propagation delays to as close to the physical limits as possible. FIBRE aimed to ensure a fairer distribution of mining opportunities, making sure that the proportion of blocks mined by a participant faithfully reflects its contribution in computing power, regardless of its position on the network.

Latency in block transmission can favor large groups of miners, which are well connected and often close to one another, to the detriment of smaller ones. This phenomenon could, over time, increase mining centralization and reduce the overall security of the system. To address this problem, FIBRE introduced error-correcting codes and the sending of additional data to offset packet loss, as well as the use of compact blocks similar to those described in BIP-0152, all operating over UDP to get around certain limitations of TCP. However, FIBRE was abandoned in 2020, mainly because of its reliance on a single maintainer and because the adoption of BIP-0152 made such a system less indispensable.
