Attack on the Lightning Network that exploits the HTLC mechanism to steal funds. It was described in 2020 by Jona Harris and Aviv Zohar. The attacker controls two nodes and opens channels with many intermediate victims. The attacker routes a large number of HTLCs from the source node to the target node through the victims, then refuses to reveal the preimage. As the timelocks approach expiration, all the victims are forced to close their channels unilaterally at the same time, which causes massive congestion of the blockchain. This congestion prevents some closing transactions from being confirmed in time. The HTLCs not claimed before expiration revert to the attacker, who can outbid the victims on fees using RBF. Unlike griefing, which temporarily freezes funds without stealing them, flood-and-loot allows actual theft. The researchers estimated that about 85 channels attacked simultaneously are enough to guarantee a profit. The attack has never been observed in real-world conditions. Proposed countermeasures include reducing the maximum number of HTLCs per channel, adopting anchor outputs to adjust fees dynamically during a force close, and shortening expiration delays.
