Hypothetical scenario on the Bitcoin system in which a malicious actor controls more than 50% of the total mining hashrate. With such dominance, the attacker can manipulate the consensus process, enabling malicious actions such as double spending, where the same bitcoin is spent once on a chain that is ultimately made obsolete, then a second time on the valid chain. Another purpose of a 51% attack is transaction censorship. However, carrying out a 51% attack requires considerable financial, human, energy, and technical resources, and makes the malicious actor likely to be discovered before the attack takes place. Although theoretically possible, a 51% attack on Bitcoin is considered very unlikely because of the decentralization of mining and the large amount of computing power deployed in 2026.

*When an attacker carries it out to destroy the value of Bitcoin, for example for a political motive or to profit from a short position, rather than to spend twice, it is called a "Goldfinger attack" (a term proposed by Kroll, Davey, and Felten in 2013).*
