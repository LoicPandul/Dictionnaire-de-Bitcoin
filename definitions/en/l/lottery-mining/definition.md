Form of solo mining in which a miner uses a low-hashrate device to try to find a block independently, without joining a pool. The probability of success is extremely low, but the reward, if a block is found, goes entirely to the miner.

The devices used are generally compact or open-source ASICs such as the Bitaxe, whose hashrate ranges from about 0.4 to 1.3 TH/s depending on the model (Ultra, Supra, Gamma). Against a network that exceeds 800 EH/s, the odds of solving a block are on the order of one in a billion per block, which comes up about every ten minutes. Some miners connect to solo mining services such as Solo CKPool or Braiins Solo, which provide them with block templates and relay valid blocks to the network, without pooling rewards.

The practice is more of a hobby than a profitable activity. Its supporters justify it by the contribution to hashrate decentralization and by the appeal of a full reward in case of success.
