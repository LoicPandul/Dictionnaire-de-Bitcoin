Digital currency service based in Costa Rica, founded by Arthur Budovsky in 2006. It allowed users to create accounts with minimal identity verification and to transfer funds denominated in “Liberty Reserve Dollars” (LR) or in euros. The service became popular for illicit transactions because of the anonymity it offered. At its peak, Liberty Reserve had more than one million users worldwide and reportedly processed about 55 million transactions.

In May 2013, the US government seized the service and indicted its operators, accused of laundering more than $6 billion. Together with the earlier shutdown of e-gold, this episode confirmed that centralized digital currency systems remain vulnerable to state intervention. Bitcoin, thanks to its decentralized architecture that spreads risk across its participants, aims to resist this kind of shutdown.
