Hypothetical scenario on the Bitcoin system in which a malicious actor controls more than 50% of the total mining computing power (hashrate), not to earn bitcoin, but to destroy Bitcoin or its value for the benefit of an interest outside its economy: preventing payments, protesting, or betting on a price drop. The term comes from a 2013 paper by Kroll, Davey, and Felten, in reference to the villain of the film *Goldfinger*. With such dominance, the attacker can manipulate the consensus process and carry out malicious actions such as the double spend, in which the same bitcoin is spent a first time on a chain that is eventually made obsolete, then a second time on the valid chain. Another goal of a Goldfinger attack is transaction censorship. However, carrying out an attack of this kind requires considerable financial, human, energy, and technical resources, and makes the malicious actor likely to be discovered before the attack takes place. Although theoretically possible, a Goldfinger attack on Bitcoin is considered very unlikely because mining is decentralized and a large amount of computing power is currently deployed.

*This attack is a special case of the 51% attack, distinguished by its purpose rather than its method.*
