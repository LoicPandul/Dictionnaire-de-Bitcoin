Zero-knowledge proof system of the SNARK type, proposed by the cryptographer Jens Groth in 2016. Groth16 stands out for the minimal size of its proofs (only three elliptic curve elements, about 200 bytes) and for its constant verification time, which makes it one of the most efficient schemes for on-chain verification. In exchange, Groth16 requires a trusted setup ceremony specific to each circuit, during which secret parameters are generated and then ideally destroyed. If those parameters were reconstructed, it would be possible to create false proofs. The RISC Zero framework uses Groth16 as its final wrapping layer to compress large STARK proofs into compact SNARK proofs. This is the mechanism the Citrea rollup relies on to publish validity proofs on the Bitcoin blockchain.
